Root causes, ranked by impact

#1

Measurement is broken end to end

The app has no tags. Meta's "sign-ups" are CTA clicks. Google Ads has no conversions. 62% of sign-ups have no source. EU visitors get no consent banner.
Impact: the algorithms learn the wrong goal, and nobody can tie the 7 paying teams to spend.

#2

Budget spread thin, on the wrong inventory

19 campaigns and ad sets on $13.5/day is $0.71 a day each. None can reach the ~50 events/week a Meta ad set needs to exit learning. Brand-term broad match, Display, Audience Network, and 34% of spend in the US for 0 sign-ups.

#3

No path from payment to "invite 2 teammates"

7 teams paid and 0 activated. After checkout come 4 pop-ups, an empty Space, and no Invite button. This is the biggest risk to month-2 renewals.

What I'm less sure of: if the 53 extra active users are invitees, #3 is partly a tracking problem, not only a product problem. The D10 SQL check settles it.

Part 3 · Experiments
5 / 7

Three experiments, judged on metrics closer to revenue than CTR, each checked against its own sample size.

E1 · Landing page → sign-up (5.8% now)
Hypothesis

Visitors drop because the hero is abstract and the CTA opens "Welcome back". A job-specific hero with real UI will lift sign-ups.

Change

Fix the CTA first (shipped to 100%, not tested). Then A/B the hero: "Lead, run your team workflow" vs "One Space per client" + UI above the fold on mobile + the line "reminder 3 days before renewal".

Metric

Server-side sign-ups ÷ unique visitors. Guardrail: pay rate.

Sample

8% → 11.2% needs 1,330 visitors per arm, ≈ 1.7 weeks.

Win / loss

Win: ≥ +25% relative, p < 0.05, pay rate not more than 3 points lower. Loss: test the offer block next.

Logic check: the variant echoes ad angle A, so randomise independently of the ad and report by angle. Check that the split is really 50/50 (sample-ratio mismatch).

E2 · Paying → activated (0% now)
Hypothesis

Teams pay, but nothing asks them to invite anyone. Making "invite 2 teammates" the first screen after payment will raise activation.

Change

A 2-step setup after checkout: name the Space, then invite 2 people (email, copy link, WhatsApp). The 4 pop-ups are muted in session 1, there's a permanent Invite button, and the payer gets nudges on day 0, 1 and 3.

Metric

% of paying teams with a Space and ≥ 2 invites within 72 h.

Sample

50/50 by team. 10% → 40% needs 32 teams per arm, and 30% → 60% needs 42. That's 3–4 weeks.

Win / loss

Win: ≥ +20 points and ≥ 95% probability (Bayesian). Loss: under +10 points after 84 teams, so test an extra-seat incentive.

Logic check: the baseline of 0% may be a tracking error, not a real 0%. The control arm measures the true baseline, so I don't assume one.

E3 · My choice: sell the job or sell the price?
Hypothesis

Angle A, "one Space per client", brings fewer but better sign-ups than angle C, the "$1" price. CTR would pick the wrong winner.

Change

A vs C, each as a static and a 9:16, in Meta's creative test (equal spend inside the one ad set). Same landing page, same CTA, utm_content per ad.

Metric

Week 1: cost per sign-up, with pay rate as a guardrail. Final: cost per paying team.

Sample

Final call at ≥ 20 payers per angle, ≈ 3 weeks.

Win / loss

Win: ≥ 30% cheaper per paying team. Make 3 new hooks on the winner. Tie: test the persona next.

Logic check: my first draft read 3 angles in 1 week. Detecting a 15% → 30% pay-rate difference needs ~120 sign-ups per arm, so I cut to 2 angles and only kill clear losers (more than 2× the cost per sign-up) early.

Part 4 · Product experience — landing page → invite a teammate (my walkthrough, 25 Nov)
6 / 7

Activation requires 2 invites, yet inviting is the hardest action to find.

1 · A new paid visitor lands on "Welcome back · Sign in"

The CTA goes to the checkout URL, so logged-out visitors are bounced to login. "Create one" is a small link at the bottom. Behind it: a 6-field form, a strict password rule, and no Microsoft SSO. The referral link also drops the ref code and the UTMs.

Fix: open "Create your team" with Google/Microsoft SSO first.

2 · 7 onboarding steps, then "Plan & billing" on the Free plan

After 6 questions, "Open workspace" lands on billing with the plan still Free, the $1 button below the fold, and a modal on top. The offer says "card at sign-up", but in the product, paying is optional and unclear.

Fix: 2 questions → checkout → Space.

3 · No visible way to invite a teammate

4 pop-ups and a 5-step tour lead to an empty Space. The sidebar has no Invite button, though the landing-page mock shows one. The only prompt is a paid banner, "invite up to 4 teammates", which doesn't fit teams of 5–25 people.

Fix: E2's 2-step setup and a permanent Invite button.

Part 4 · Ad concept
7 / 7

Sell the client workflow, not "another all-in-one app".

1:1 static for Meta Feeds and ChatGPT Ads. A 9:16 cut-down follows for Stories and Reels.

Audience

Leads of 5–25-person agencies and remote teams in the Philippines who work with overseas clients. They are problem-aware: they feel the chaos, but aren't shopping for a tool.

Copy

Headline (41 / 50): One Space per client. Files, chat, tasks.

Description (96 / 100): Stop chasing the approved version across apps. Expiring client links. Team plan: $1 first month.

Hook: "Wait… which version did the client approve?" in the customer's own words.

Visual brief

A before / after split. Before, in grey: the brief, the "see attached" chat, an email thread and a link still open. After: real Drumee UI with files, the chat thread and a share card with expiry and password. Bottom: "$1 first month · then $29/month" and the CTA "Start your team". No competitor names and no unmeasured numbers.

Why this angle (Motion)

ClickUp's longest-running Meta ads (238–242 days live) are "One app to replace them all" us-vs-them statics. That consolidation angle is taken. Drumee's provable difference is one Space per client and links it controls. Angle C ("$1") is the challenger in E3.
