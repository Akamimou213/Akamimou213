How it works

At sign-up we store UTMs, gclid, fbc/fbp, GA client ID and consent on the user record. That keeps the source alive through login, SSO and checkout. Backend and billing events flow through server-side GTM in the EU (Stape) to Meta CAPI, Google Ads and GA4, with one event_id so browser and server events dedupe.

Why renewals are handled differently

The $29 renewal lands on day 30, outside Meta's 7-day click window. So Meta gets it for lookalikes and value signals, and Google gets it as an offline conversion (gclid stays valid for 90 days). The backend stays the source of truth, reconciled daily, with an alert when the gap exceeds 15%.

Part 3 · Experiments
5 / 7

Three experiments, each judged on a metric closer to revenue than CTR, and sized to the traffic we have.

E1 · Landing page → sign-up (5.8% today)
Hypothesis

Cold visitors leave because the hero is generic ("run your team workflow"), the product sits below the mobile fold, and the CTA opens a login page. If the page repeats the ad's promise and shows the real product, more visitors will sign up.

Change

Baseline fix to 100% first: CTA → "Create your team" with SSO.
A/B: today's hero vs a message-matched hero. Its headline repeats the ad's hook and promise ("Which version did the client approve? One Space per client."), with real UI above the fold on mobile, Google/Microsoft buttons on the page, and "Reminder 3 days before renewal · cancel in one click" under the CTA.

Metric

Server-side sign-ups per unique visitor (experiment ID carried into the app). Guardrails: trial rate, mobile vs desktop.

Sample

8% → 11.2% at 80% power needs 1,330 visitors per arm, ≈ 9 days at ~300 visitors/day.

Win / loss

Win: ≥ +25% relative, p < 0.05, trial rate not lower. Loss: keep the control and test the offer block next.

Design note: the variant bundles 4 changes on purpose. At this traffic, one-change tests take months. If it wins, we unbundle to find the driver. Before building, 50 PostHog session replays confirm where visitors drop.

E2 · Trial → activated team (0% today)
Hypothesis

Teams pay, then land in billing, 4 pop-ups and an empty Space with no Invite button. If inviting 2 teammates becomes the first screen after payment, activation will rise.

Change

A 2-step setup after the $1 payment: (1) name the Space (pre-filled: "Client: …"), (2) invite 2 people by email, copy link or WhatsApp. Pop-ups muted in session 1. A permanent Invite button. Nudges to the payer on day 0, 1 and 3.

Metric

% of trial teams with a Space + ≥ 2 invites within 72 h (the brief's definition). Quality guardrail: ≥ 1 invite accepted.

Sample

50/50 by team. 10% → 40% needs 32 teams per arm (≈ 3 weeks). Read on D31 with a Bayesian check, and keep running past the offer if volume is short.

Win / loss

Win: ≥ +20 points and ≥ 95% probability. Loss: under +10 points, so test a seat incentive.

Watch-outs: "invites sent" can be gamed (dummy emails), so accepted invites are tracked too. And 2 invites is the brief's definition, not proven: on D40 we check that activated teams really renew more.

E3 · My choice: pay first or onboard first?
Hypothesis

Only 24% of sign-ups pay because the $1 step comes after 7 onboarding screens, below the fold. If checkout comes right after the account is created, as the offer promises ("add a card to start"), more sign-ups will start a trial.

Change

Control: today's order (sign-up → 7 questions → billing). Variant: sign-up → $1 checkout → the rest of onboarding. Same price, same copy. Randomised independently of E2 (a 2×2 split), so each test reads cleanly.

Metric

Sign-up → trial within 24 h. Guardrails: activation at 72 h, refund requests.

Sample

Sized for a big effect: 24% → 40% needs ~130 sign-ups per arm, ≈ 2–3 weeks at ~60 per arm a week. Starts D12, read at D28.

Win / loss

Win: ≥ +15 points of trial rate, p < 0.05, activation not lower. This also unlocks StartTrial as Meta's optimisation event. Loss: keep today's order and simplify the billing screen.

Why this one: it moves the event the ads optimise for. More trials per sign-up means Meta reaches 50 trials a week sooner, so every dollar from week 3 buys paying teams, not just accounts.

1 · New visitors land on "Welcome back · Sign in"

The CTA goes to the checkout URL, so logged-out visitors are bounced to login. "Create one" is a small link, and behind it is a 6-field form with no Microsoft SSO. The referral link loses the ref code and the UTMs.

2 · 7 steps, then billing on the Free plan

The offer says "add a card to start", but the $1 step comes after onboarding, below the fold, under a modal. Paying feels optional. That's why only 24% of sign-ups pay, and why Meta can't optimise for trials yet.

3 · No visible way to invite

4 pop-ups and a tour lead to an empty Space, and the sidebar has no Invite button (the landing-page mock shows one). The only prompt is a paid banner, "invite up to 4 teammates", for teams of 5–25.

Part 4 · Ad concept — creative strategy
7 / 7

Sell the client handoff, not "another all-in-one app".

1:1 for Meta Feeds and ChatGPT Ads. A 9:16 version for Stories and Reels uses the same layout, stacked.

Audience

Agency leads in the Philippines running 8–15-person creative, marketing or VA teams for US/AU clients. Paid in USD. Client work is spread across Drive, Messenger and email.

Insight

Overseas clients judge an agency by how organised the handoff looks. Losing "the approved version" costs trust, not just time.

Concept

Hook in the customer's own words ("Wait… which version did the client approve?"), then the promise, then real Drumee UI as proof: one client Space with files, the discussion threaded on the budget file, and the meeting in the same place. Format: a product-hero static, the most common format among Meta ads running 60+ days.

Copy

Headline (41/50): One Space per client. Files, chat, tasks.

Description (96/100): Stop chasing the approved version across apps. Expiring client links. Team plan: $1 first month.

Primary text: Client files in one app, feedback in another, approvals buried in email. Drumee gives every client one Space: files, chat and tasks together. Team plan: $1 for your first month.

Why this angle (Motion)

ClickUp's longest-running Meta ads (238–242 days live) all say "one app to replace them all", so that space is taken. Drumee owns a narrower, provable promise: one Space per client. No competitor names and no unmeasured numbers. The CTA states the real offer ($1, not "free"), so ad, landing page and checkout match.
