- Dean: « Les banques deviennent de plus en plus difficiles à travailler avec. … quatre à cinq mois après l'acquisition, une

How they talk: operational, specific, unemotional. They count in **weeks** (« 3 à 4 semaines »), **doors** (« portes »),
**files** (« le fichier »), **logins** (« se connecter à chaque banque »). The emotion is time and control, not money.

## 3. Outside sources: the world around the persona

- Québec law: a landlord can't require post-dated cheques; tenant and landlord must agree on the payment method
  ([TAL](https://www.tal.gouv.qc.ca/fr/etre-locataire/paiement-du-loyer), [Éducaloi](https://educaloi.qc.ca/capsules/le-paiement-du-loyer/)).
  So collection is a patchwork of cheques, Interac transfers and debits, tenant by tenant.
- Interac transfers are now the most common way rent is paid in Québec, per a 2019 CORPIQ survey cited by
  [Sylvestre Painchaud](https://spavocats.ca/publications/paiement-du-loyer-par-virement-interac-qui-assume-les-risques-de-fraude/);
  CORPIQ warns of [fraud aimed at rent transfers](https://www.corpiq.com/fr/nouvelles/2096-preventions-des-fraudes-visant-les-paiements-de-loyer.html)
  and itself [promotes pre-authorised rent debits](https://www.corpiq.com/fr/nouvelles/2388-simplifiez-la-collecte-des-loyers-avec-les-paiements-preautorises-de-proprio-expert.html)
  (Proprio Expert, which TOMSO powers).
- CORPIQ calls rent deposited into an account the manager fully controls « un feu rouge »
  ([Radio-Canada](https://ici.radio-canada.ca/info/long-format/2050805/gestionnaire-immeuble-travaux-facture)); condo law
  sources say each syndicate's money belongs in its own account ([RGCQ brief](https://rgcq.org/media/knzjanic/memoire_rgcq_groupe-de-travail_2010.pdf),
  [Condolegal](https://www.condolegal.com/gestion/sousfiches/1136-la-charge-financi-re)). Separate accounts are the norm, so
  the number of accounts grows with every building.
- Condo cheques need co-signers, and « each Corporation has challenges ensuring the cheques are signed in a timely manner »
  ([CCI-National](https://cci.ca/resource-centre/view/109?filter=&words=)); paper AP « invoices get lost on desks or buried
  in email threads » ([SparcPay](https://sparcpay.com/hoa-condo-boards-balancing-transparency-with-efficiency-in-ap/), vendor).
- Loi 16 is fully in force since 14 Aug 2025: maintenance logbooks, contingency-fund studies, more reporting for managers
  ([Protégez-Vous / Notaires](https://www.protegez-vous.ca/partenaires/chambre-des-notaires-du-quebec/carnet-d-entretien-etude-du-fonds-de-prevoyance-les-nouvelles-obligations-sont-la-!-1re-partie),
  [Regisco](https://www.regisco.ca/post/loi-16-reglement-copropriete-2025)): more work lands on the same condo managers.
- Software reviews of the tools they use today repeat the same complaints: reconciliation problems, online payments slow
  to set up, money held at the start of the month (Buildium reviews coded by [Reallyo](https://www.reallyo.com/blog/buildium-pricing-and-reviews),
  [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/47428/Buildium-Property-Management-Software/reviews/?page=20); a landlord on
  [BBB](https://www.bbb.org/us/ma/boston/profile/computer-software/buildium-llc-0021-28244/complaints): « April 1 is coming
  soon and I have not received March rent »); « Lack of integration with banking » as a reason to leave Condo Manager
  ([Capterra CINC](https://www.capterra.com/p/143799/CINC-Systems/reviews/)); PAD providers' « 5 business day » hold
  ([PaymentReview on Rotessa](https://paymentreview.com/reviews/rotessa/), [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/170269/Rotessa/)).

## 4. Patterns

1. **The calendar is the enemy.** Everything piles onto one date: rent on the 1st, NSF a few days later, owners waiting
   for deposits. TOMSO's own copy keeps returning to « le 1er du mois ». It's a monthly, predictable, shared trauma.
2. **The work is bridging, not deciding.** The pain is copy-pasting between software and banks, logging into portal after
   portal, checking transactions one by one. Skilled people do clerical glue (« font le raccord manuellement »).
3. **Growth multiplies the glue.** One more building = one more account, more signatories, more logins. The case study's
   real story is « sans augmenter son personnel ».
4. **They don't want to change their stack.** Every FAQ answer reassures: keep your software, keep your banks. The fear is
   a migration, not the price.
5. **Control is non-negotiable.** Money belongs in the owner's or syndicate's own account; approvals and signatories are
   legal duties. Automation that hides money is a red flag; automation that shows exceptions is a relief.
6. **They speak in time, not money.** « 3 à 4 semaines », « en quelques jours », « en quelques minutes », « des heures plutôt que des semaines ».

## 5. Insights (the human truths we'll build on)

- **I1 — « Le 1er du mois n'est pas une date, c'est une crise mensuelle. »** Rental teams lose the first days of every month
  to cheques, portals and NSF before a single dollar is in. TOMSO's promise is the anticlimax: « juste une journée comme
  toutes les autres ». The most ownable emotion here is boredom, the good kind.
- **I2 — Their job title says manager; their month says data entry.** Smart people are doing the bridge between two
  systems that don't talk. Relief = « Vous vous occupez de ce qui compte. Pas du reste. »
- **I3 — Every new door is welcome, every new bank account isn't.** Growth should feel like winning, but each mandate adds
  a portal, signatories and weeks at the bank (Dean: « Ça prenait trop de temps »).
- **I4 — They don't want less control, they want fewer things to control.** « Gérez seulement les exceptions » reframes
  automation as more control, not less.
