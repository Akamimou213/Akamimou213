# How the Gut Health Score works

**Title tag (55 chars):** `How the Gut Health Score Works: Inputs, Window & Limits`

> **In short:** The Gut Health Score is a daily number in the Get Regular app that summarizes your own logs. Per our privacy policy, it's "based on your past 7 days of entries, weighted toward the most recent data" [6]. It's a way to see your trend over time, not a medical test. It hasn't been validated in a clinical study, it can't diagnose anything, and it doesn't compare you with other people.

## How does the 7-day window work? {#window}

**The score looks back over your past 7 days of entries and counts recent days more than older ones** [6]. That's the whole published definition. In practice it means:

- **One unusual day doesn't define your score.** It's blended with the rest of the week.
- **Changes show up within days.** Because recent entries count more, a shift this week moves the score sooner than an equal shift from six days ago.
- **Logs older than 7 days don't count** toward today's score. Your Backlog keeps the full history.
- **Backdated entries count.** You can log entries up to 7 days in the past, so a forgotten log can still be added to the window. **[CONFIRM: backdated entries recalculate past and current scores]**

## What raises or lowers the score? {#what-moves-it}

**[CONFIRM: direction and size for each input. The table below is a DRAFT of directions to check with product; render only rows product confirms.]**

| What you log | Likely effect (draft, **[CONFIRM]**) | What the research says |
|---|---|---|
| Stool in the middle of the Bristol scale (types 3–4) | Raises it | Types 3–5 are often grouped as "normal" in research [2]. The app's streak uses 3–4 (see [below](#streak)) |
| Hard stool (types 1–2) or loose stool (types 6–7) | Lowers it | Stool water rises from hard (1–2) to normal (3–5) to loose (6–7) types [2]; harder stool tends to go with slower transit [1] |
| Going within your usual range | Raises it or no effect **[CONFIRM]** | 3 a day to 3 a week covers most people with self-reported normal habits [3] |
| Several days with no bowel movement | Lowers it **[CONFIRM]** | — |
| Frequent symptoms | Lowers it **[CONFIRM]** | — |

**Two things that don't move it the way you might expect:**
- **Your score isn't graded against other people.** A score that's lower than a friend's doesn't mean your gut is less healthy. Normal bowel habits vary widely. In one Bristol population study, only about 40% of men and 33% of women had a regular once-a-day rhythm [5].
- **A "perfect" week isn't the target.** Even among US adults reporting normal bowel habits, 10% of men fell outside types 3–5, and women ranged more widely (types 2–6 covered 90%) [3].

## What the Gut Health Score isn't {#what-it-isnt}

| It isn't… | Why |
|---|---|
| **A diagnosis or medical test** | It can't tell you whether you have IBS, constipation, an infection or any other condition. A clinician makes those calls, sometimes with tests |
| **Clinically validated** | Get Regular hasn't run a study checking the score against clinical measures or outcomes. Our clinical advisor's input isn't the same as validation. See [who reviews our content](/science/clinical-review) |
| **A measure of your microbiome** | It's built from what you log, not from a stool sample. Even for actual microbiome tests, a 2025 international expert consensus found little evidence of usefulness in clinical practice [7] |
| **A population norm** | There's no standard "gut health score" in medicine, and we don't have data to compare you with others |
| **An alarm** | A good score doesn't rule anything out, and the app won't flag red-flag symptoms. See a doctor for blood in your stool, black or tarry stool, unexplained weight loss, severe or constant pain, or a change in bowel habits that lasts more than a few weeks [8] |

## How does the gut-health streak work? {#streak}

**A gut-health streak counts consecutive days with stool in the Bristol 3–4 range** (app release notes). Days you log as no-go days don't break the streak.

**Why we're careful with streaks:** 3–4 is a narrower band than most research uses. Studies often group types 3–5 as normal [2], and many healthy people regularly pass type 2 or type 5 [3]. Breaking a streak doesn't mean something is wrong. It's a motivation tool, not a health threshold.

## What is the Nutrition Score? {#nutrition-score}

**The Nutrition Score is a daily score for the nutritional quality of what you logged,** including gut-relevant compounds such as fiber, polyphenols and prebiotics. You can also check a food's score before you eat it.

**[CONFIRM: one-line method — which nutrients count, whether fiber is weighted more, and whether it's AI-estimated from photos/text. Until confirmed, render only the sentence above.]**

**Limits:** the score depends on AI estimates of ingredients and portions from your photos or descriptions, and published tests of general-purpose AI on meal photos found large portion and nutrient errors [9]. It isn't medical nutrition therapy and doesn't know your allergies unless you've told the app. More on [how our AI works](/science/how-our-ai-works#research).
