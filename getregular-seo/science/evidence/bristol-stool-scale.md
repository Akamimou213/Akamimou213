# What the research says about the Bristol Stool Scale

> **Short answer:** The Bristol Stool Form Scale is a 7-point scale for stool shape and consistency, and it works as a rough guide to how fast stool moves through the gut. In 66 volunteers, changes in stool type tracked changes in transit time better than bowel movement frequency or stool weight did [1]. People rated stool models correctly 81% of the time, but they disagreed more near the borders between types [2].

**The 7 types, in our own words:** 1 hard, separate pellets · 2 a hard, lumpy log · 3 a formed log with a cracked surface · 4 a smooth, soft log · 5 soft pieces with defined edges · 6 mushy, loose pieces · 7 liquid. See the full [Bristol stool chart](/tools/bristol-stool-chart).

## The evidence at a glance

| Study | Type | Who | What it found | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lewis & Heaton, *Scand J Gastroenterol* 1997 [1] | Volunteer study; transit changed with senna and loperamide, measured with radiopaque markers | 66 volunteers | At baseline, stool form correlated with whole-gut transit time (r = −0.54) better than frequency (r = 0.35) or stool output (r = −0.41). Change in form tracked change in transit best (r = −0.65) | Volunteers, not patients; correlations are moderate; one center |
| Blake, Raker & Whelan, *Aliment Pharmacol Ther* 2016 [2] | Validation study | 169 healthy adults and 19 people with IBS-D; 86 volunteers rated 26 stool models | Stool type correlated with measured stool water (rho = 0.491). Healthy mean 3.7 vs IBS-D mean 5.0. 81% of model ratings correct (κ = 0.78); same type on a hidden repeat 76% of the time (κ = 0.72), but only 63% for type 2 and 62% for type 3 | Accuracy tested on models, not real stool; small IBS-D group; fewer than 80% correct for types 2, 3, 5 and 6 |
| Degen & Phillips, *Gut* 1996 [3] | Physiology study (scintigraphy and radiopaque markers) | 32 healthy volunteers on standardized diets, each studied twice | Hard stools went with slow colonic transit, loose stools with fast colonic transit. Stool form was not related to stomach emptying or small-bowel transit. Women had harder stools | Small sample; one center |
| Mitsuhashi et al., *Am J Gastroenterol* 2018 [4] | National survey (NHANES 2009–2010) | 4,775 US adults who reported normal bowel habits | 90% of men reported types 3–5; for women the 90% range was types 2–6 | Self-report; "normal" was self-defined; one survey cycle |

## What this means

- **It measures form, and form is a stand-in for speed.** Harder types go with slower colonic transit and looser types with faster [1][3]. It says little about the stomach or small bowel [3].
- **"Normal" is a range, not one type.** One study grouped types 3–5 as normal [2]. In US adults with normal habits, women's usual range ran wider than men's [4].
- **Expect some fuzziness at the edges.** Telling type 2 from 3, or 5 from 6, is where people disagree most [2]. A pattern across many entries tells you more than any single rating.
- **Changes beat snapshots.** The scale was most informative when tracking change in the same person over time [1].

## What's still uncertain

- Whether training or clearer wording would make ratings more accurate. The validation authors suggested testing this [2].
- Where "normal" ends. Sources draw the line differently, especially around type 5 [2][4]. [CONFIRM reviewer: type-5 framing]
- Most data come from healthy volunteers or small patient groups [1–3]. How well self-ratings track transit in older adults or people taking gut-active medicines is less clear.
- The correlations are moderate [1], so the scale can't replace a transit test when a clinician needs one.

## Track it

Rate every bowel movement on the Bristol scale for 2 weeks, along with time of day. Look at your typical type and how often you land at the hard or loose ends, not at single days.
