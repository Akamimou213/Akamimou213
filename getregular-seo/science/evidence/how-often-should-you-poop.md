# What the research says about how often you should poop

> **Short answer:** There's no single right number. In a US national survey of 4,775 adults who called their bowel habits normal, 95.9% reported between 3 and 21 bowel movements a week, which supports the common "three a day to three a week" rule [1]. Once a day is the most common pattern, but in a UK population study only 40% of men and 33% of women had a regular daily cycle [2].

## The evidence at a glance

| Study | Type | Who | What it found | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mitsuhashi et al., *Am J Gastroenterol* 2018 [1] | National survey (NHANES 2009–2010) | 4,775 US adults who reported normal bowel habits | 95.9% reported 3–21 bowel movements a week. Male sex, higher education, higher income, fewer than 2 daily medications and high fiber intake were linked with normal habits | Self-report; only people who felt "normal", so it describes what's common, not what's healthy |
| Heaton et al., *Gut* 1992 [2] | Prospective population study | 838 men and 1,059 women in East Bristol, UK (72.2% of a random sample) | Once daily was the most common habit but still a minority. A regular 24-hour cycle in 40% of men and 33% of women. A third of women went less than daily; 1% once a week or less | Most people recorded only three bowel movements; one city; early 1990s |
| Walter et al., *Scand J Gastroenterol* 2002 [3] | Postal survey | Random sample of 2,000 adults aged 31–76 in one Swedish county; 80.5% responded | 67.8% reported one bowel movement a day; 95.6% fell between 3 a day and 3 a week. Fewer than 3 a week: 4.3% of women, 1.7% of men. About 20% of women considered themselves constipated | Self-report; one region |
| Connell et al., *Br Med J* 1965 [4] | Population survey | Two UK population samples | Early study of how much bowel habit varies; commonly credited as the source of the "three a day to three a week" range | PubMed lists no abstract; we have not checked its figures against the full text [CONFIRM reviewer] |
| Lewis & Heaton, *Scand J Gastroenterol* 1997 [5] | Volunteer study with transit markers | 66 volunteers | Frequency correlated with whole-gut transit time (r = 0.35), but stool form correlated better (r = −0.54) | Volunteers; moderate correlations |

## What this means

- **A wide range is common.** About three a day to three a week covers most adults in both the US and Swedish surveys [1][3].
- **Daily isn't required.** Most people in the Bristol study were not on a regular daily cycle [2].
- **Women tend to go less often.** More women than men reported fewer than 3 a week in Sweden [3], and a third of women in Bristol went less than daily [2].
- **Frequency is only half the picture.** Stool form tracked transit time better than frequency did [5]. Log your [stool type](/science/evidence/bristol-stool-scale) as well as your count.

## What's still uncertain

- These studies show what is *common*, not what is *healthy*. Falling outside the range doesn't by itself mean something is wrong, and falling inside it doesn't rule out a problem [1].
- Most data are self-reported, and some are decades old [2][3][4].
- The US survey found links between frequency and sex, income, medications and fiber, not causes [1].
- A change from your own usual pattern may matter more than where you sit in the population range. [CONFIRM reviewer: wording]

## Track it

Count bowel movements and note stool type for 2 weeks. Then compare your weekly total with the 3–21 range and see whether your pattern is steady or swings.
