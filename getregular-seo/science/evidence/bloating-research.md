# What the research says about bloating

> **Short answer:** Bloating is a feeling of trapped gas, pressure or fullness; distension is a measurable increase in belly size, and the two often but not always go together [3][5]. Bloating is common (13.9% of 88,795 surveyed Americans had it in the past week) and has several possible causes, including food intolerances, constipation or slow transit, a more sensitive gut and an unusual reflex of the belly and diaphragm muscles [2][3][6]. The 2023 AGA clinical practice update offers best-practice advice, but it is expert advice without formal evidence grades [1].

## The evidence at a glance

| Study | Type | Who | What it found | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Moshiree, Drossman & Shaukat, AGA Clinical Practice Update, *Gastroenterology* 2023 [1] | Expert review with 15 best-practice advice statements | Adults with belching, bloating or distension | Use Rome criteria to diagnose primary bloating. Imaging or endoscopy only with alarm features, worsening symptoms or an abnormal exam. Probiotics "should not be used" for bloating. A GI dietitian should preferably supervise diets such as low FODMAP | No systematic reviews, so statements "do not carry formal ratings" of evidence quality or strength |
| Oh, Chey & Spiegel, *Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol* 2023 [2] | Nationally representative survey | 88,795 Americans | 13.9% had bloating in the past 7 days. Women and people with IBS, chronic constipation or other GI conditions had higher odds. 58.5% of those with bloating never sought care for it | Self-reported, one-week snapshot |
| Lacy, Cangemi & Vazquez-Roque, *Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol* 2021 [3] | Review | Chronic bloating and distension | Bloating = trapped gas, pressure, fullness. Distension = measurable increase in girth. Causes include food intolerances, a past gut infection, disordered gut sensation, delayed transit and an abnormal reflex. "No regimen has been consistently successful" | Narrative review |
| Malagelada, Accarino & Azpiroz, *Am J Gastroenterol* 2017 [4] | Review | Bloating and distension | Rule out organic (structural or disease) causes first. Clues come from diet history, bowel movement frequency and stool consistency | Narrative review |
| Houghton et al., *Gastroenterology* 2006 [5] | Ambulatory girth measurement over 24 h | 50 women with IBS, 20 healthy women | 48% of IBS patients had distension beyond the healthy controls' 90% range, up to 12 cm. Bloating and distension only tracked each other in IBS with constipation | Small, women only, older Rome II criteria |
| Villoria et al., *Am J Gastroenterol* 2011 [6] | Lab provocation study | 20 patients with bloating, 15 healthy people | With the same gas load in the colon, patients developed more distension, with the diaphragm contracting instead of relaxing | Small, artificial gas load |

## What this means

- **Feeling bloated and looking bloated are different things.** About half of people with IBS who feel bloated also measurably swell, and the link is strongest with constipation [5]. That's why it helps to note both [3].
- **There isn't one cause.** Food intolerances, constipation, a sensitive gut and muscle reflexes can all play a part [3][6]. Your diet and bowel pattern give clues [4].
- **Don't start with probiotics.** The AGA update advises against them for bloating. It favors a dietitian-guided diet, treating constipation if present, and brain-gut therapies such as CBT or hypnotherapy [1]. Diaphragmatic breathing is used when the diaphragm reflex is involved [1].
- **You're not alone, and it's worth raising.** Most Americans with recent bloating never sought care for it [2]. A doctor can rule out causes such as celiac disease with a blood test [1].

## What's still uncertain

- Bloating treatments have limited evidence. The AGA update says so directly, and its advice isn't formally graded [1].
- No treatment works for everyone [3]. Finding the mechanism in a given person "is not always feasible" [4].
- The girth and diaphragm studies are small [5][6].
- How Rome V's bloating criteria differ from Rome IV needs the full text [7].

## Track it

For 2–3 weeks, log meals, stool type (Bristol scale) and when bloating starts. Note whether your belly looks swollen or just feels full. A log gives your doctor more to work with than memory.
