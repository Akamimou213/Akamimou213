> **In short:** Get Regular hasn't published any research yet, and we have no results to share. Since version 2.0.0 (August 2026), the app has an optional "Research consent" setting so that, in the future, people who choose to can contribute de-identified data to studies of everyday bowel habits, food and symptoms. This page explains what that consent covers, how we'd protect your data, and what we'll publish and when. We'll update it as things happen, including when results are disappointing.

## Why does a tracking app want to do research? {#why}

**Because much of what we know about normal bowel habits comes from questionnaires and short diaries, and people's day-to-day logs could add to it.**

- The largest US study of normal bowel habits used survey answers from about 4,800 adults (NHANES 2009–2010) [1].
- A classic Bristol population study asked people to record just three consecutive bowel movements [2].
- When people recall their gut symptoms on a questionnaire, the answers often don't match what they wrote in a daily diary, especially for complex symptoms such as pain relieved by a bowel movement [3].

People who log in Get Regular record stool type, frequency, meals and symptoms day by day, over weeks or months. With consent, de-identified and pooled, that kind of data might help answer practical questions, for example how much bowel habits vary from week to week in the same person. We don't know yet whether our data will be good enough to answer them. App users aren't a random sample of the population, logging is uneven, and stool types come from AI photo analysis or self-report. Any study we publish will say so.

**What we won't do:** call anything "our research" or "our data shows" until a study is finished and its method is public.

## How would my data be protected? {#privacy}

**Research would use only de-identified, pooled data from people who opted in.** These are the principles we're committing to. **[CONFIRM: legal and privacy review of each principle before publishing]**

1. **Consent first.** Only data from people who turned on research consent. Nothing collected before you opted in is used. **[CONFIRM]**
2. **De-identified.** Names, emails, account IDs and device IDs are removed before analysis. Photos and free-text notes and chat messages are never part of a research dataset. **[CONFIRM]**
3. **Aggregate results only.** We publish group-level numbers, never one person's logs. Results for small groups are suppressed so no one can be singled out. **[CONFIRM: minimum group size, e.g., no cell with fewer than N people]**
4. **Kept apart from marketing.** Research data isn't shared with advertisers or marketing tools. Our privacy policy already says we don't sell, rent or share your personal or health information with advertisers [4].
5. **Independent ethics review for studies.** Any study we publish in a journal will go through an independent ethics review (an institutional review board, or IRB) or an approved exemption, and will say so. **[CONFIRM: plan for IRB review]**
6. **Your rights still apply.** The rights in our privacy policy (including CCPA/CPRA, the Washington My Health My Data Act and GDPR) apply to research data too [4]. **[CONFIRM with legal]**

"De-identified" doesn't mean risk-free. Health data can sometimes be re-identified when combined with other data, which is why we pool it and suppress small groups. Full detail: [privacy policy](/legal/privacy-policy).

## Are the free tools validated? {#tool-validation}

**No. Our free [bloat profile quiz](/tools/why-am-i-bloated) and [stool chart](/tools/bristol-stool-chart) are self-checks, not validated questionnaires.** The quiz matches your answers to the bloating patterns described in the American Gastroenterological Association's 2023 clinical practice update, and sends anyone with a warning sign to a doctor before anything else. It gives no score, has no clinical cut-offs and can't diagnose anything. The stool chart uses the 7-type idea from Lewis & Heaton 1997 in our own words and drawings.

**What validating the quiz would take** (a plan, not something we've done) **[CONFIRM: whether and when the team will run this]**:

| Step | Question it answers | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Clinical review of the questions, patterns and warning signs against the AGA 2023 update | Do the questions and patterns match current clinical guidance? | Pending clinical review |
| 2. Safety audit of the warning-sign routing | Does every answer with an alarm feature reach the "see a doctor" result? | Automated tests in place; clinical sign-off pending |
| 3. Test-retest | Do people get the same top pattern when nothing has changed? | Not started |
| 4. Compare with 7-day experiment outcomes | Among people who opt in to research, does the suggested experiment change their logged bloating more often than chance? | Not started; needs research consent |
| 5. Publish the method and results | Can others check our work? | Not started |

Until those steps are done and published, we won't call the quiz validated, accurate or clinically meaningful. The in-app Regular Score is a separate measure, described on [our gut health score page](/science/gut-health-score).

## For researchers {#researchers}

**If you study bowel habits, IBS, diet or digital health and want to talk, contact us.** We're especially interested in:
- Independent validation of AI stool-form and meal-photo classification.
- Study design and de-identification review.
- Validating simple self-check tools against clinical measures.

**Contact:** **[CONFIRM: research contact email, e.g., research@getregular.com; until confirmed use hello@getregular.com with "Research" in the subject]**
