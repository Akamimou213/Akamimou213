## How does review work at Get Regular? {#how-review-works}

**Every health page shows who wrote it, who reviewed it and when.** If a page hasn't been reviewed by a clinician, it isn't published. It stays a draft.

| Step | Who | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Write | Get Regular editorial team **[CONFIRM: named author(s) and their background]** | Draft built from guidelines and studies in our [evidence library](/science/evidence). Every number is checked against the study's PubMed abstract |
| 2. Fact-check | Editorial team | Product claims checked against our internal fact sheet (App Store listing, privacy policy, terms). Anything we can't confirm is removed |
| 3. Clinical review | A qualified clinician **[CONFIRM: Dr. Schopis or another named reviewer, per page]** | Checks medical accuracy, red-flag advice and wording. Pages show "Reviewed by [name], [credential] on [date]" only when that review actually happened |
| 4. Publish | Editorial team | Page goes live with its review date |
| 5. Re-review | Editorial team + clinician | **[CONFIRM: review cycle, e.g., every 12 months or when a guideline changes]**. Sources are re-checked each time |

**App logic is separate from articles.** Reviewing an article doesn't mean the reviewer checked how the app's scores or AI work, and the reverse. The advisor card below lists each separately.

## Our editorial standard {#standard}

These are the rules every article, tool and science page follows. The full internal version is the Get Regular editorial standard.

- **Answer first, in plain language.** Every page opens with a short, direct answer you could quote on its own.
- **Every health claim has a source.** We cite clinical guidelines first (for example ACG and AGA guidelines, Rome V criteria), then systematic reviews, trials and studies. Government and medical sources (NIDDK, NHS) cover red-flag advice. See [how we choose evidence](/science/evidence#how-we-choose).
- **We read the source before we use its numbers.** Numbers come from the study abstract or full text, re-checked at each review.
- **No invented numbers.** No made-up statistics, user counts, ratings, testimonials or "normal" ranges.
- **Patterns, not causes.** We say a food or habit "may be associated with" symptoms. We never say an app "finds your triggers" or "diagnoses" anything.
- **We only describe features we can confirm.** Product facts come from the App Store listing, our privacy policy and terms. Unconfirmed features aren't mentioned.
- **Red flags on every symptom page.** Each symptom page says when to see a doctor (for example blood in the stool, black or tarry stool, unexplained weight loss, symptoms that wake you at night, or a lasting change in bowel habits).
- **Fair comparisons.** When we compare apps, we state facts with the date we checked and say that Get Regular publishes the article.
- **Dated and reviewed.** Every page shows when it was last updated and, if a clinician reviewed it, who and when.

## What clinical review doesn't mean {#limits}

- **It isn't clinical validation.** Get Regular hasn't published a study testing its scores or AI against clinical measures. See [our research program](/science/research).
- **It doesn't make the app a medical device.** Get Regular is a wellness tracking app. It doesn't diagnose, treat or prevent any condition.
- **It doesn't cover every AI output.** The AI generates insights, suggestions and chat answers for each user. No clinician reviews those one by one. See [how our AI works](/science/how-our-ai-works#limits).

## How to report an error {#report-an-error}

**If you think something on our site or in the app is wrong, tell us.** We read every report.

- **Email:** hello@getregular.com
- **Subject line:** "Content error" (website), "Evidence error" (a citation), or "AI error" (something in the app)
- **Include:** the page URL or the app screen, what you think is wrong, and a source if you have one. Please don't email stool photos; describe the issue instead.

**What happens next:** **[CONFIRM: response time, e.g., "We reply within 5 working days."]** If we agree, we fix the page, update its "last updated" date and, for a meaningful health correction, add a short correction note at the bottom of the page saying what changed and when. **[CONFIRM: correction-note policy]**

**If it's about your health, not our content,** contact your doctor. If it's urgent, call your local emergency number.
