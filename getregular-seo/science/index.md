> **In short:** Get Regular helps you log four things doctors already use to understand digestion: stool form, how often you go, what you eat and your symptoms. Our articles and tools are built on published clinical guidelines and studies, every one linked to PubMed. Get Regular hasn't run or published studies of its own yet, its scores and AI haven't been clinically validated, and the app doesn't diagnose anything. This section shows our sources, our methods and our limits.

## What does Get Regular track, and why? {#what-we-track}

**We track the four things that describe your digestion day to day, because each has research showing it's worth recording.**

| What you log | How | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Stool form** | Bristol Stool Scale type 1–7, from a photo or picked by hand | Stool form is a practical guide to how fast stool moves through your gut. In a study at the Bristol Royal Infirmary, changes in stool form tracked changes in measured transit time more closely than changes in how often people went [1] |
| **How often you go** | Each bowel movement is time-stamped; you can log no-go days | It gives your normal range a baseline. Of US adults who say their bowel habits are normal, 95.9% go between 3 times a day and 3 times a week [2]. Knowing your own range makes a change easier to spot |
| **Meals** | Photo or text, with editable ingredients | Food is a common suspect. In a Swedish study of 197 people with IBS, 84% linked their symptoms to at least one food [3]. What people suspect isn't always the real cause, so a meal log lets you test a hunch instead of guessing |
| **Symptoms** | One tap when they happen (bloating, pain, urgency and more) | Memory is unreliable. In population and primary-care samples, what people recalled on a symptom questionnaire often didn't match what they had recorded in a daily diary [4] |

The app also lets you log drinks, supplements, medications, time on the toilet, notes and period dates, which add context to the four core logs. See [how our AI works](/science/how-our-ai-works#inputs) for the full list.

## How does Get Regular's AI work? {#ai}

**The AI reads what you log and summarizes it. It doesn't diagnose.** It estimates ingredients and nutrients from meal photos and descriptions, suggests a Bristol type from stool photos (or you pick the type yourself), and turns your recent logs into daily scores, a Weekly Digest of possible patterns and answers in Gut Check chat. To do this, your logged content is sent to OpenAI's API without your name, email or account ID, per our privacy policy. Photos aren't permanently stored on our servers.

Published studies have tested AI on stool and meal photos with mixed results. Those studies tested other systems, not ours, and Get Regular hasn't published its own accuracy study.

## Does Get Regular do its own research? {#research}

**Not yet. We have no results to share.** Since version 2.0.0, the app has an optional "Research consent" setting so that people who choose to can contribute de-identified data to future studies of bowel habits, food and symptoms. Our research page explains what that consent covers, how data would be protected, and what we'll publish first. We'll publish the method before any results.

## What we don't claim {#dont-claim}

*(Render as a bordered box, visible without scrolling on desktop below the "Research" section; never collapsed.)*

> **What we don't claim**
>
> - **We haven't published our own studies.** Every study on this site was run by independent researchers. None of them tested Get Regular.
> - **Our scores and AI aren't clinically validated.** Input from a gastroenterologist isn't the same as a validation study.
> - **Get Regular doesn't diagnose, treat or prevent any condition.** It isn't a medical device and can't tell you whether you have IBS, an intolerance or any other condition.
> - **We don't publish accuracy figures for our AI.** Not until we've tested it and published the method.
> - **A pattern isn't a cause.** Insights describe possible patterns in your own logs, to test and discuss with a clinician.
> - **Our scores aren't population norms.** They summarize your logs and don't rank you against anyone else.
> - **We don't measure your microbiome** or use lab tests.
>
> If you have blood in your stool, black or tarry stool, unexplained weight loss, severe or constant pain, or a lasting change in bowel habits, see a doctor.
