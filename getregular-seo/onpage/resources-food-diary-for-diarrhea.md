## 1. Answer box

> **Quick answer:** A food diary for diarrhea logs what you eat and drink (with times), when loose stools happen, their Bristol type (5–7), urgency and how many a day — plus stress, alcohol, caffeine and medicines. The time between a meal and a loose stool is the key clue. Keep it 2 weeks. See a doctor first if diarrhea lasts more than 2 days with fever, blood, black stools or dehydration, or keeps coming back for 4 weeks or more.

## 2. Intro

Diarrhea after meals is miserable and hard to plan around — and it's tempting to blame whatever you ate last. Sometimes that's right. Often the answer is a meal from several hours earlier, a drink, a medicine, stress, or a normal reflex that's simply stronger in you.

This guide shows what to log, a timing guide to help read it, a filled example week, the red flags that mean "see a doctor now", and a free printable.

**In this guide:** [Is it diarrhea?](#definition) · [What to log](#what) · [Timing guide](#timing) · [Example week](#example) · [When to get help](#red-flags) · [Printable](#printable)

## 3. Key takeaways

- Diarrhea = loose, watery stools **3+ times a day**, or more often than normal for you [1].
- **Timing** between eating and urgency is your best clue.
- Log **Bristol type, urgency and count per day**, not just "bad day".
- **Fluids first** — dehydration is the main short-term risk [1][2].
- Diarrhea lasting **4 weeks or more** is chronic and should be investigated [1][3].

## 4. Is it diarrhea? {#definition}

**H2:** What counts as diarrhea, and when is it chronic?

**Answer:** Diarrhea is passing loose, watery stools three or more times a day, or more often than is normal for you [1]. Acute diarrhea usually lasts less than a week; persistent diarrhea lasts 2–4 weeks; chronic diarrhea lasts at least 4 weeks and may come and go [1].

| Term | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Acute | Usually < 1 week, goes away on its own | [1] |
| Persistent | > 2 weeks and < 4 weeks | [1] |
| Chronic | ≥ 4 weeks; continuous or on-and-off | [1] |
| Chronic (UK specialist definition) | Persistent change from your norm with Bristol types 5–7 **and** increased frequency for more than 4 weeks | [3] |
| Functional diarrhea (Rome IV) | Loose or watery stools in > 25% of bowel movements for 3 months, without predominant pain or bloating (otherwise IBS-D is considered) | [4] |

In the US, up to about 5% of people may have chronic diarrhea [1].

## 5. What to log {#what}

**H2:** What should a diarrhea food diary include?

**Answer:** Meal and drink times and contents; each bowel movement's time, Bristol type and urgency; the number per day; any accidents or near-misses; and context: stress, sleep, alcohol, caffeine, sugar-free sweeteners, medicines and recent illness or travel.

| Field | How to log it | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Meal / drink + time | Include fat content (fried, creamy), dairy, spice, alcohol, caffeine, sugar-free gum/sweets | Common drivers: dairy, sugar alcohols (sorbitol, mannitol, xylitol), alcohol, fatty or spicy food [1][5][6] |
| BM time | Clock time | Links meals to stools |
| Bristol type | 5, 6 or 7 (or other) | Defines loose vs watery [3][7] |
| Urgency | 0 none · 1 hurry · 2 rush · 3 couldn't wait | Urgency is a core diarrhea symptom [2] |
| Count per day | Total BMs | ≥ 6 loose stools a day needs prompt advice [2] |
| Night-time BM? | Y/N — did it wake you? | Nighttime diarrhea is an alarm feature [8] |
| Cramping / pain 0–10 | With time | Pain linked to BMs points toward IBS-D [9] |
| Fluids | Cups; oral rehydration solution? | Dehydration risk [1] |
| Medicines | Antibiotics, metformin, magnesium, GLP-1 medicines, laxatives | Several cause loose stools [1] — see [GLP-1 diarrhea tracking](/blog/glp1-diarrhea-tracking) |
| Stress 0–10 / sleep | Daily | Stress can trigger IBS flares [10]; see [stress diarrhea](/blog/stress-diarrhea) |
| Context | Travel, ate out, someone at home ill, new food | Infection clues |

## 6. Timing guide {#timing}

**H2:** How soon after eating does diarrhea start — and what can the timing suggest?

**Answer:** Very fast urgency (minutes to an hour) often reflects the gastrocolic reflex, the normal signal that makes your colon move after eating [11]. Loose stools a few hours after a specific food fit better with an intolerance such as lactose [5]. Symptoms from a stomach bug or food poisoning usually come with other signs — vomiting, fever, people around you sick.

| Timing after eating | Pattern in your diary | Can be associated with | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minutes – ~1 h | Urgent BM soon after most meals, especially big or fatty ones, or first thing in the morning after coffee/breakfast | Gastrocolic reflex; frequent urgent episodes can occur with IBS | [11] |
| Within a few hours | Loose stools, gas and bloating after milk, ice cream, soft cheese | Lactose intolerance | [5] |
| A few hours, lasting hours–days | After a specific food, repeatedly | Food intolerance | [12] |
| Hours, with vomiting/fever, others ill | Sudden onset, 1–2 days | Viral gastroenteritis or food poisoning — usually stops in 5–7 days | [1][13] |
| No link to meals; at night | Wakes you from sleep | Alarm feature — see a doctor | [8] |

*Timing ranges are illustrative, not diagnostic.* Deeper dives: [diarrhea right after eating](/blog/diarrhea-after-eating) · [urgent bowel movements in the morning](/blog/urgent-bowel-movement-morning) · [why coffee makes you poop](/blog/coffee-poop).

## 8. How to read your diary

**H2:** How do you find what's causing your diarrhea?

1. **Mark every Bristol 6–7** and every urgency score ≥ 2.
2. **Note the gap** from the last meal (and the meal before that).
3. **Group by timing**: within an hour, a few hours, or the next morning.
4. **Tally suspects** in each group (fat, dairy, alcohol, caffeine, sweeteners, spice, a medicine).
5. **Check good days** for the same suspects.
6. **Count per day and per week**, and whether any were at night.
7. Take the summary to a doctor, especially if it's gone on 4 weeks or more. For IBS with diarrhea symptoms, the ACG suggests celiac blood tests and a fecal calprotectin test to rule out other conditions [14].

## 9. When to get help {#red-flags}

**H2:** When does diarrhea need a doctor?

**Talk with a doctor right away if you (or an adult in your care) have:** [2]
- diarrhea lasting more than 2 days
- a high fever
- six or more loose stools a day
- severe pain in the abdomen or rectum
- stools that are black and tarry or contain blood or pus
- frequent vomiting
- signs of dehydration: extreme thirst, dry mouth, peeing less, dark urine, dizziness or light-headedness
- a change in mental state, such as unusual irritability or lack of energy

**Also see a doctor if:**
- diarrhea keeps coming back or lasts 4 weeks or more [1][3]
- it wakes you from sleep [8]
- you're losing weight without trying [9]
- you're pregnant, over 65, on antibiotics or immunocompromised — keep in touch with your doctor [1]

**Children and babies** dehydrate faster — get medical advice early [2].

More: [changes in bowel habits that need a doctor](/blog/when-to-see-doctor-bowel-changes) · [yellow diarrhea in adults](/blog/yellow-diarrhea).

## 12. FAQ

**H2:** Food diary for diarrhea FAQ

**Why do I poop right after I eat?**
Eating triggers the gastrocolic reflex, which makes your colon move to make room — it can start within minutes [11]. A strong or frequent version is common in IBS. Log the timing to see your pattern: [diarrhea right after eating](/blog/diarrhea-after-eating).

**How do I know which food is causing my diarrhea?**
Log meals and bowel movements with times for 2 weeks, then look for foods that repeatedly come before loose stools at a similar gap — and are absent on good days. Test one suspect at a time with a clinician's input.

**How long after eating does food poisoning start?**
It varies with the cause — from hours to days. Diarrhea from a stomach bug usually stops within 5–7 days [13]. Get help sooner for any red flag in §9.

**Can stress cause diarrhea?**
Stress and anxiety can trigger IBS symptoms, including diarrhea [10]. Rate stress daily in your diary to check. See [stress and loose stools](/blog/stress-diarrhea).

**Is it normal to have diarrhea after dairy?**
It's common with lactose intolerance; symptoms usually start within a few hours [5]. See [diarrhea after dairy](/blog/diarrhea-after-dairy).

**When is diarrhea chronic?**
When it lasts 4 weeks or more [1]. Chronic diarrhea should be checked by a doctor.
