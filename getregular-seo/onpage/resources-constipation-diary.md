## 1. Answer box

> **Quick answer:** A constipation diary tracks each bowel movement (time and Bristol type), straining, the feeling of incomplete emptying, no-go days, plus what affects them: fiber, fluids, activity, toilet routine, medicines and stress. Keep it for 2 weeks. It shows whether you're truly constipated — often defined as fewer than three bowel movements a week or hard, difficult stools — and what tends to come before your hardest days.

## 2. Intro

"Constipated" can mean not going for days, going daily but straining, or never feeling done. Those are different problems, and a diary is the fastest way to tell which one you have — and to show a doctor if simple changes don't help.

## 3. Key takeaways

- Constipation is about **frequency and how stools feel to pass**, not just days without going [1][2].
- Track **Bristol type, straining and incomplete emptying**, not only the count.
- Fiber, fluids, movement, routine, medicines and stress are the usual levers — log them [2][3].
- **2 weeks** of data is enough to see a pattern and to show a doctor.
- Blood, weight loss, constant pain, vomiting or not passing gas need a doctor right away [2].

## 4. Am I constipated? {#definition}

**H2:** What counts as constipation?

**Answer:** Constipation means you may have fewer than three bowel movements a week; hard, dry or lumpy stools; stools that are difficult or painful to pass; or a feeling that not all stool has passed [1]. Bowel habits vary a lot between people, so only you know what's normal for you [1].

- **How common?** About 16 in 100 US adults have constipation symptoms, and about 33 in 100 adults aged 60 and over [1]. Worldwide, roughly 1 in 10 adults meet Rome IV criteria for functional constipation [4].
- **Normal varies:** in a UK study of nearly 1,900 adults, fewer than half had a regular once-daily habit, and a third of women went less than daily [5].
- **Doctors' definition of chronic functional constipation (Rome IV)** uses symptoms over the last 3 months — for example straining, lumpy or hard stools (Bristol 1–2) or a feeling of incomplete emptying in more than a quarter of bowel movements, or fewer than three spontaneous bowel movements a week [6].

| You might say… | What to track |
|---|---|
| "I haven't gone in 3 days" | No-go days, frequency per week — see [haven't pooped in days?](/blog/havent-pooped-in-days) |
| "I go, but it's hard work" | Bristol type, straining, toilet time |
| "I never feel finished" | Incomplete emptying (Y/N) after each BM |
| "It swings between hard and loose" | Bristol type over time — see [constipation then diarrhea](/blog/constipation-then-diarrhea) |

## 5. What to track {#what}

**H2:** What should you write in a constipation diary?

**Answer:** For every bowel movement: time, Bristol type, straining, incomplete emptying and toilet time. Every day: fiber foods, fluids, activity, medicines/supplements, stress and anything that broke your routine.

| Field | How to log it | Why |
|---|---|---|
| BM time | Clock time; tick "no BM today" on no-go days | Frequency and your natural timing (many people go in the morning [5]) |
| Bristol type 1–7 | Closest match on the [Bristol chart](/resources/bristol-stool-chart) | Types 1–2 = hard; stool form tracks transit time [7] |
| Straining | None / some / a lot | Part of how constipation is defined [1][6] |
| Incomplete emptying | Y/N | Same [1][6] |
| Toilet time | Minutes | Long sits can signal difficult evacuation |
| Fiber foods | List them (oats, fruit, veg, beans, whole grains) | Adults are advised 22–34 g fiber/day; increase gradually [8] |
| Fluids | Glasses/cups or ml | Low fluids can contribute [2][3] |
| Activity | Minutes walked/exercised | Inactivity can contribute [2][3] |
| Routine | Ignored an urge? Travel? Time change? | Ignoring the urge and routine changes are common causes [2][3] |
| Medicines & supplements | Name, dose (iron, calcium/aluminum antacids, opioids, some antidepressants, laxatives) | Many can worsen constipation [2] |
| Stress 0–10 | Once a day | Stress and anxiety are listed causes [3] |
| Bloating / pain 0–10 | Once or twice a day | Often travels with constipation [3] |
| Cycle day / pregnancy | Optional | Constipation is common in pregnancy and around periods [2][3] |

## 7. How to read your diary {#read}

**H2:** How do you read a constipation diary?

1. **Count BMs per week** and no-go days.
2. **Count hard stools** (types 1–2) and strained or incomplete BMs. More than a quarter is worth discussing with a doctor if it's been going on for weeks [6].
3. **Look at the 1–3 days before your hardest days**: fiber, fluids, activity, ignored urges, travel, new medicines.
4. **Find your natural window** — many people go after breakfast (the gastrocolic reflex makes the colon more active after eating) [9]. Building a toilet routine around it can help [2]; see [how to get on a regular poop schedule](/blog/regular-poop-schedule).
5. **Change one thing at a time** (e.g., add soluble fiber gradually) and keep logging for 1–2 weeks to judge it [8].

**What a diary can't tell you:** whether a medical condition (such as an underactive thyroid, celiac disease, a pelvic floor problem or, rarely, a bowel obstruction) is involved — that's for a doctor [3].

## 8. What helps (and what the guidelines say)

**H2:** What helps with constipation?

**Answer:** Start with diet and routine changes; you may notice a difference within a few days, though it can take a few weeks [2].

- **Diet:** gradually increase fiber; add oats, wheat bran or linseed; include fruits that contain sorbitol (apples, apricots, grapes, raspberries, strawberries); drink plenty of fluids [2]. Add fiber slowly — 2–3 g a day — to avoid gas and bloating [8]. See [can fiber make constipation worse?](/blog/fiber-constipation)
- **Routine:** go at a regular time and place, don't delay when you feel the urge, and try resting your feet on a low stool so your knees are above your hips [2].
- **Move:** a daily walk or run can help you go more regularly [2].
- **Pharmacy:** a pharmacist can suggest a laxative if lifestyle changes aren't enough; most work within 3 days and are for short-term use unless a doctor advises otherwise [2]. For chronic constipation, US gastroenterology societies' 2023 guideline makes strong recommendations for several over-the-counter and prescription options (for example polyethylene glycol) and a conditional recommendation for fiber — choices to make with your clinician [10].

## 9. When to see a doctor {#red-flags}

**H2:** When is constipation serious?

**See a doctor right away if you're constipated and have:** [1]
- bleeding from your rectum or blood in your stool
- constant pain in your abdomen
- inability to pass gas
- vomiting
- fever
- lower back pain
- weight loss without trying

**Book a GP or doctor visit if:** [2]
- constipation isn't improving with treatment, or you're regularly constipated or bloated
- you're constipated and feel tired all the time
- a medicine (such as an opioid painkiller) may be causing it — don't stop it without advice
- you notice a sudden change in your bowel habits, or have a family history of colon or rectal cancer [1]

**Watch for overflow:** long-term constipation can cause fecal impaction, where watery stool leaks around a blockage and looks like diarrhea [2].

## 12. FAQ

**H2:** Constipation diary FAQ

**How long is too long without pooping?**
Fewer than three bowel movements a week is one common definition of constipation [1]. See a doctor promptly if no BM comes with vomiting, constant pain, fever, or inability to pass gas [1]. More: [haven't pooped in days?](/blog/havent-pooped-in-days)

**Can I have bowel movements every day and still be constipated?**
Yes. Hard, lumpy stools, straining or feeling you haven't fully emptied can count as constipation even with daily BMs [1].

**How do I get regular bowel movements?**
Regular meal times, fiber added gradually, enough fluids, daily movement and not ignoring the urge — especially after breakfast — help many people [2][9]. Track for 2 weeks to see what works for you.

**Does cheese cause constipation?**
It may for some people, especially as part of a low-fiber diet. Test it with your diary: see [does cheese cause constipation?](/blog/cheese-and-constipation)

**Can stress cause constipation?**
Stress, anxiety and depression are listed among common causes [3]. Log stress daily to check your own pattern: [can stress cause constipation?](/blog/can-stress-cause-constipation)

**Why do I get constipated when I travel?**
Changes in routine, food, fluids and activity all play a part [2][3]. See [travel constipation](/blog/constipated-after-travel).
