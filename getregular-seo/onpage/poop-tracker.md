## 2. Why track your poop at all?

**H2:** Why track your bowel movements?
**Answer:** Because "normal" is personal, and memory is unreliable. Stool form is one of the simplest everyday signals of how fast things move through your gut: in a classic study, Bristol stool type tracked whole-gut transit time better than how often people went [1]. A few weeks of logs show your own baseline — and make changes easier to spot and explain.

- Healthy bowel habits vary widely. In a study of almost 1,900 adults in Bristol, UK, fewer than half had a regular once-a-day pattern [2].
- Clinicians use stool form to describe constipation and diarrhea. For example, Rome IV criteria for functional constipation include hard or lumpy stools (Bristol types 1–2) in more than a quarter of bowel movements [3].
- When you see a doctor, "how often, what type, since when" is exactly what they'll ask [4].

## 6. Honest limits

**H2:** What the poop tracker won't do

> **Get Regular doesn't diagnose anything.** It shows your logs and possible patterns; a clinician interprets them.
> - **AI can be wrong.** Photo classification can misread lighting, angle or mixed types. If a result looks off, choosing the type manually is always an option.
> - **Scores summarize, they don't judge.** A lower score means your recent logs moved away from the Bristol 3–4 range or other inputs changed — not that something is wrong with you.
> - **It's not a FODMAP database** and doesn't replace a dietitian.
> - **It can't see what you don't log.** Gaps make patterns less reliable.

**Red-flag box (always visible, styled as alert):**
**See a doctor promptly if you notice:** blood in or on your stool, or black, tarry stools · constant or severe abdominal pain · unexplained weight loss · vomiting, fever, or being unable to pass gas with constipation · diarrhea lasting more than 2 days or six or more loose stools a day · diarrhea that wakes you at night [8] · a lasting change in your usual bowel habit, especially new symptoms later in life or with a family history of colon cancer [5][6][7]. More: [bowel changes that need a doctor](/blog/when-to-see-doctor-bowel-changes).

## 7. Privacy

**H2:** What happens to your stool photos?

- **You don't have to take any.** Manual Bristol entry works without a photo.

## 9. FAQ

**H2:** Poop tracker FAQ

**Is there an app to track bowel movements?**
Yes. Get Regular is a poop tracker app for iPhone and iPad: log each bowel movement by Bristol type (photo or manual), time, toilet time and notes, alongside meals and symptoms. Apple Health doesn't offer a dedicated bowel-movement log — see [our Apple Health guide](/blog/apple-health-poop-tracking). **[CONFIRM: wording of the Apple Health clause after the C7 post is verified.]**

**Do I need to photograph my poop?**
No. Pick the Bristol type yourself. Photos are optional and, per the privacy policy, aren't permanently stored on Get Regular's servers.

**How many times a day should you poop?**
There isn't one right number. A common reference range is anywhere from three times a day to three times a week, and in a large UK study most people did not go exactly once a day [2]. What matters more is a change from your own normal — which is what a log shows. More: [how often should you poop?](/blog/how-often-should-you-poop)

**What Bristol type is "normal"?**
Types 3 and 4 are generally considered the easiest to pass, and the app's streak counts days in that range. Types 1–2 lean toward constipation and types 5–7 toward loose stools [1][3]. See the [Bristol stool chart](/resources/bristol-stool-chart).

**Can I log a day when I didn't go?**
Yes. No-go days are logged and don't break your streak — useful for tracking constipation. See [haven't pooped in days?](/blog/havent-pooped-in-days)

**Can I show my log to my doctor?**
Yes, with the Premium Doctor Report: a PDF with your bowel patterns, Bristol tracking and symptom frequency. A CSV export isn't documented. **[CONFIRM]**

**Is it on Android?**
No — iPhone and iPad only (as of 19 Oct 2026).
