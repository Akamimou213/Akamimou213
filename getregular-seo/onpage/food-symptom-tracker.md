## 2. Why a food and symptom tracker (not just a food diary)?

**H2:** Why track food and symptoms together?
**Answer:** Because the link between a meal and a symptom is rarely obvious in the moment. Food-intolerance symptoms often start a few hours after eating and can last hours or days [1][2], while the urge to poop can start within minutes of a meal for unrelated reasons [3]. Logging meals, symptoms and bowel movements on one timeline is how you see which is which.

- The NHS suggests that people with suspected food intolerance may be asked to keep a food and symptoms diary, and that people with IBS keep a diary of what they eat and any symptoms [1][4].
- Home "food intolerance tests" aren't recommended by the NHS; there's limited evidence they give accurate results [1]. A diary of what actually happens to you is a more useful starting point for a conversation with a clinician.
- Cutting foods without guidance can backfire. Make changes with a doctor or dietitian, especially for children or if you're cutting several foods [1].

## 5. Timing: why "yesterday's lunch" matters

**H2:** How long after eating do symptoms show up?
**Answer:** It depends on the cause, which is why the timeline matters more than the last meal.

| What you notice | Typical timing | What it can be associated with | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Urge to poop during or soon after a meal | Within minutes to about an hour | The gastrocolic reflex — a normal response to eating; frequent, urgent episodes can occur with IBS | [3] |
| Bloating, gas, loose stools after dairy | Within a few hours | Lactose intolerance | [5] |
| Bloating, gas, diarrhea, tummy pain after a specific food | Usually a few hours; can last hours to days | Food intolerance | [1] |
| Swelling of lips/tongue/throat, wheeze, hives soon after eating | Minutes | Food **allergy** — an emergency, not something to track | [1] |

## 7. Honest limits

**H2:** What this tracker won't do

> - **It doesn't diagnose** food intolerance, allergy, IBS or anything else.
> - **Patterns aren't proof.** A food that shows up before symptoms may be a coincidence — especially foods you eat every day. Treat patterns as hypotheses to test. See [correlation or coincidence?](/blog/food-symptom-patterns)
> - **AI can misread a photo** (hidden ingredients, sauces, portion size). Edit the ingredient list when it's wrong.
> - **It's not a FODMAP database** and doesn't tell you a food's FODMAP content. For that, Monash University's FODMAP app is the reference tool; for a guided low-FODMAP diet, work with a dietitian.
> - **It's not for allergies.** If you have allergy symptoms (swelling, wheezing, hives), seek urgent care.

**Red-flag box:** **See a doctor if you have:** blood in your stool or black, tarry stools · unexplained weight loss · symptoms that wake you at night · persistent vomiting or severe abdominal pain · bloating with a lump or swelling in your tummy · symptoms that keep coming back despite diet changes [4][6][7].

## 8. Privacy

**H2:** What happens to your meal photos and symptom logs?
- Food photos, descriptions, symptoms, medications and supplements are sent to OpenAI's API for analysis. The privacy policy states only anonymized content is sent (no names, emails or account IDs) and OpenAI doesn't train on API inputs.
- Photos are compressed for analysis and not permanently stored; only the results are saved. Prefer no photos? Type your meals.
- Your health data isn't sold or shared with advertisers.
- Delete your account in the app; data is permanently removed within 30 days.

## 10. FAQ

**H2:** Food and symptom tracker FAQ

**Is there an app to track food and bowel movements together?**
Yes. Get Regular logs meals (photo or text), symptoms and bowel movements (Bristol type, photo or manual) on one timeline, on iPhone and iPad.

**How long should I keep a food and symptom diary?**
Many guides suggest at least 2 weeks so that patterns can repeat; delayed symptoms and infrequent foods need longer. Our [14-day method](/blog/how-to-identify-food-triggers) walks through it.

**Do I have to enter every ingredient?**
No. Snap a photo or type the meal and the AI fills in ingredients you can edit.

**Will the app tell me my trigger foods?**
It shows possible patterns to test. It can't prove a food causes a symptom — a structured test, ideally with a dietitian, is how you check.

**How long after eating do food intolerance symptoms start?**
Usually a few hours after eating, and they can last hours or days, according to the NHS [1]. Urgency within minutes of a meal is more often the gastrocolic reflex [3].

**Can I use it for an elimination diet?**
You can log an elimination and reintroduction, and Premium Experiments guide simple one-food tests. For a full low-FODMAP program, work with a dietitian. See [how to track an elimination diet](/blog/elimination-diet-tracking).

**Can I share my log with my doctor or dietitian?**
Yes — the Premium Doctor Report is a PDF that includes possible food–symptom correlations. See [how to share your logs with your doctor](/blog/share-food-symptom-log-with-doctor).
