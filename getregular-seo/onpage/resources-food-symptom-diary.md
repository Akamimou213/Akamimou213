## 1. Answer box

> **Quick answer:** A food and symptom diary is a log of everything you eat and drink — with the time and portion — next to any symptoms, when they started and how bad they were (0–10), plus your bowel movements. Keep it daily for at least 2 weeks. Then look for symptoms that repeatedly follow the same food within a similar time window, and discuss those possible triggers with a doctor or dietitian.

## 2. Intro

Most food diaries fail for the same reason: they record *what* you ate but not *when* — or *when* the symptoms started. Without timing, a diary can't tell a coffee that hits within minutes from a dairy-heavy lunch that catches up with you three hours later.

This guide gives you a free template built around timing, a filled-in example week, and a simple way to read it. It works for bloating, gas, pain, loose stools, constipation, reflux or "I just feel off after eating."

## 3. Key takeaways

- Record **times**: meal time, symptom onset time and bowel movement time.
- Rate symptoms **0–10** so you can compare days.
- Keep going for **at least 2 weeks**; foods need to repeat before a pattern means anything.
- A pattern is a **hypothesis to test**, not a diagnosis [1].
- Don't cut out several foods at once without guidance [1].

## 4. Why keep a food and symptom diary?

**H2:** Why does a food and symptom diary help?

**Answer:** Because food reactions are usually delayed and mixed up with other things — stress, sleep, your cycle, illness. A diary gives you and your clinician evidence instead of guesses.

- **Food intolerance symptoms are usually delayed.** The NHS says they typically start a few hours after eating and can last hours or days [1]. Lactose intolerance symptoms can start within a few hours of milk products [2].
- **Not everything after a meal is about that meal.** The gastrocolic reflex can trigger the urge to poop within minutes to about an hour of eating, regardless of what you ate [3].
- **Clinicians use diaries.** The NHS notes you may be asked to keep a food and symptoms diary when a food intolerance is suspected, and recommends a food and symptom diary for people with IBS [1][4].
- **Home intolerance tests aren't a shortcut.** The NHS doesn't recommend shop-bought food intolerance tests: evidence for their accuracy is limited, and they may push you to avoid foods unnecessarily [1]. See [are food intolerance tests worth it?](/blog/food-intolerance-test-vs-food-diary)

> **Food allergy is different.** Swelling of the lips, tongue or throat, wheezing, or trouble breathing after eating is an emergency — call 911 (or your local emergency number) [1]. A diary is for intolerance-type symptoms, not allergic reactions. For allergist visits, see [food allergy diary](/blog/food-allergy-diary).

## 5. What to record: the 10 columns {#columns}

**H2:** What should a food and symptom diary include?

**Answer:** Ten columns: date, time, meal/drink, portion, symptom, onset time, severity 0–10, bowel movement (Bristol type), stress/sleep, and notes. Cycle day is an optional 11th.

| # | Column | How to fill it | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Date | One page per day | Tue 14 Oct |
| 2 | Time | Clock time you started eating/drinking | 12:40 |
| 3 | Meal / drink | Foods, sauces, cooking method, drinks | Chicken burrito bowl — rice, black beans, cheese, sour cream, salsa; iced tea |
| 4 | Portion | S / M / L, or cups | L |
| 5 | Symptom | Bloating, gas, pain, nausea, reflux, urgency, loose stool, headache, fatigue… | Bloating |
| 6 | Onset time | When the symptom began | 15:30 |
| 7 | Severity 0–10 | 0 none → 10 worst imaginable | 6 |
| 8 | Bowel movement | Time + Bristol type 1–7 (see the [Bristol stool chart](/resources/bristol-stool-chart)) | 17:10 · type 6 |
| 9 | Stress 0–10 / sleep (h) | Once per day | 5 / 6 h |
| 10 | Notes | Ate out, ate fast, period, travel, illness, new medicine | Ate at desk, rushed |
| (11) | Cycle day | Day 1 = first day of period | Day 3 |

**Tips that make the diary usable:**
- **Write within an hour.** Memory rewrites meals fast.
- **List hidden ingredients**: sauces, dressings, garlic, onion, sweeteners, milk in coffee.
- **Log "no symptoms" too** — a dash in the symptom column is data.
- **Log medicines and supplements** (including antibiotics, iron, magnesium, laxatives), because several affect bowel habits [5].

## 6. How long to keep it {#how-long}

**H2:** How long should you keep a food and symptom diary?

**Answer:** At least 2 weeks of every meal and symptom. For delayed or occasional symptoms, or foods you eat only now and then, 3–4 weeks gives a clearer picture.

| Situation | Suggested length | Why |
|---|---|---|
| General "what's bothering me?" | 2 weeks | Most regular meals repeat at least twice |
| Symptoms that come every few days | 3–4 weeks | Need several episodes to compare |
| Symptoms that may follow your cycle | 4+ weeks | Covers a full cycle |
| Before a doctor or dietitian appointment | 2 weeks minimum | Enough for a useful summary |
| During an elimination/reintroduction | Throughout, plus 1 week after | To compare before, during and after [6] |

**Keeping it up:** keep the page where you eat, log at the same moments (after each meal and each bathroom trip), and photograph restaurant meals to fill in later.

**How to read this week (possible patterns only):**
- Bloating rated ≥ 5 followed the three largest dairy meals (Tue burrito bowl with cheese and sour cream, Thu mac & cheese, Fri pizza), each about **2.5–3 hours** later, and two were followed by a type 6 stool.
- Gas after lentil soup on Saturday — beans and lentils contain carbohydrates gut bacteria ferment into gas [7]; one episode isn't a pattern yet.
- Latte mornings didn't produce symptoms on their own (Wed had none after a plain breakfast; Tue/Thu symptoms came after lunch).

## 8. How to read your diary {#read}

**H2:** How do you find food triggers in a diary?

**Answer:** Work backwards from each symptom over a set time window, count which foods repeat, and check those foods against your symptom-free days. Something that shows up before most bad days *and* rarely before good days is worth testing.

**Method (numbered steps):**
1. **Highlight every symptom ≥ 5/10.**
2. **Set windows.** For each symptom, list what you ate in the previous 0–1 hour, 1–6 hours and 6–24 hours.
3. **Tally suspects** across all highlighted symptoms.
4. **Check good days.** For each suspect, how often did you eat it on days with no symptoms? Daily staples will look suspicious by coincidence.
5. **Check dose.** Was it the large portion, or any amount?
6. **Check context.** Stress, poor sleep, cycle day, alcohol, eating fast, illness.
7. **Pick one suspect and plan a test** with your clinician: remove it for a set period while logging, then reintroduce it and watch for the same pattern [1][6].

**Timing cheat-sheet**

| Symptom timing after eating | Often associated with | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Minutes to ~1 hour: urge to poop | Gastrocolic reflex (normal response to eating) | [3] |
| Within a few hours: bloating, gas, loose stool after dairy | Lactose intolerance | [2] |
| A few hours, lasting hours–days | Food intolerance generally | [1] |
| Minutes: swelling, hives, wheeze | Food allergy — emergency | [1] |

Deeper dive: [How long after eating do food intolerance symptoms start?](/blog/delayed-food-intolerance-symptoms) · [Correlation or coincidence?](/blog/food-symptom-patterns)

**Before you cut anything out:** don't go gluten-free before celiac testing — a gluten-free diet can affect test results [8]. And don't remove several foods without advice from a GP or dietitian, especially for children [1].

## 10. When to see a doctor

**H2:** When should you see a doctor instead of just tracking?

See a doctor if symptoms keep coming back [1], and promptly if you have:
- blood in your stool, or black, tarry stools [9]
- unexplained weight loss [9][10]
- persistent vomiting, severe abdominal pain, or a swelling or lump in your tummy [10]
- difficulty swallowing, or reflux that's frequent or getting worse
- diarrhea that wakes you at night [11]
- symptoms after age 45–50 that are new for you, or a family history of colon cancer, celiac disease or inflammatory bowel disease [12]

Allergy signs (swelling, wheezing, trouble breathing) need emergency care [1]. **[CONFIRM with reviewer: add a citation for the swallowing/reflux bullet, or remove it.]**

## 14. FAQ

**H2:** Food and symptom diary FAQ

**What does a food diary look like?**
A table with one row per meal or drink: time, what you had, portion, and any symptom with its onset time and 0–10 severity, plus bowel movements and a line on stress and sleep. See the example week above.

**Does keeping a food diary actually help?**
It helps you and your clinician see patterns you'd otherwise miss, and the NHS recommends one for suspected intolerance and IBS [1][4]. It doesn't diagnose anything on its own.

**How long after eating do food intolerance symptoms start?**
Usually a few hours, and they can last hours or days [1]. That's why the onset-time column matters.

**What's the difference between a food intolerance and a food allergy?**
An intolerance is difficulty digesting a food; it can be unpleasant but isn't dangerous. An allergy involves the immune system and can cause swelling, wheezing or anaphylaxis — an emergency [1].

**Should I write down drinks and snacks too?**
Yes — coffee, alcohol, fizzy drinks, sugar-free gum and snacks are common culprits that are easy to forget [7][13].

**Can I use a food diary for bloating?**
Yes; this template doubles as a bloating diary. See [bloating after eating](/resources/bloating-after-eating) for bloating-specific timing and causes.
