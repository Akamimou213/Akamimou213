    You're not imagining it

    1 in 7 Americans.Bloated in the past week.

    It's common

    In a survey of nearly 89,000 Americans, about 1 in 7 had been bloated in the past week, and most had never sought care.

    It's measurable

    In women with IBS, belly girth increased by up to 12 cm over a day. That's distension, not "just weight".

    It's often a reflex

    After meals, the diaphragm can push down and the belly wall relax, even without much extra gas. It can be treated.

    Why am I so bloated all the time?

    The common causes, which pattern fits, and when bloating needs a doctor.

    Short answer

    Constant bloating is most often linked to how your gut handles food and gas: certain carbohydrates (lactose, fructose, fructans, sugar alcohols), constipation, IBS or a sensitive gut, swallowed air, hormonal shifts, and a belly-wall reflex after meals [1]. Bloating that's persistent or worsening, or comes with weight loss, bleeding, vomiting or early fullness, needs a doctor.

    When to see a doctor about bloating

    • Weight loss without trying, repeated vomiting, or blood in stool or vomit
    • New or worsening belly pain, fever, or symptoms that wake you at night
    • A family history of GI cancer, celiac disease or IBD [1]
    • For women, especially 50 or older: persistent bloating, feeling full quickly, pelvic or belly pain, or needing to pee more often or urgently [4]

    Questions

    Is bloating the same as belly fat?

    No. Bloating and distension come and go, often flatter in the morning and bigger by evening. A belly that's the same size all day and doesn't change with meals or bowel movements is worth checking with a doctor.

    Why only one change at a time?

    Cutting many foods at once makes it impossible to tell what helped, and can affect nutrition. Test one suspect for about a week or two. A dietitian can guide a low-FODMAP trial [1]. See what the research says about bloating.

    Where are my answers saved?

    Only in this browser, so your profile is still here when you come back. Nothing is sent to us unless you ask for an email and tick the consent box.

    Do I need tests?

    Most people don't need scans or scopes. Tests are for warning signs, recent worsening or an abnormal exam [1].
