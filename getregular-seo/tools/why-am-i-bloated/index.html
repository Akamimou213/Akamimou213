Why am I so bloated all the time?

Short answer: constant bloating is most often linked to how your gut handles food and gas. Common causes are certain carbohydrates (lactose, fructose, fructans, sugar alcohols), constipation, IBS or a sensitive gut, swallowed air, hormonal shifts, and a belly-wall reflex after meals [1]. Bloating that's persistent or worsening, or that comes with weight loss, bleeding, vomiting or early fullness, needs a doctor.

You're not imagining it

In a survey of nearly 89,000 Americans, about 1 in 7 had been bloated in the past week, and most had never sought care [2]. Visible distension is measurable: in women with IBS, belly girth increased by up to 12 cm over a day [3].

When to see a doctor about bloating

  • Weight loss without trying, repeated vomiting, or blood in stool or vomit
  • New or worsening belly pain, fever, or symptoms that wake you at night
  • A family history of GI cancer, celiac disease or IBD [1]
  • For women, especially 50 or older: persistent bloating, feeling full quickly, pelvic or belly pain, or needing to pee more often or urgently [4]

Frequently asked questions

Is bloating the same as belly fat?

No. Bloating and distension come and go, often flatter in the morning and bigger by evening. A belly that's the same size all day and doesn't change with meals or bowel movements is worth checking with a doctor.

Should I cut out lots of foods?

Test one suspect at a time for about 2 weeks. Cutting many foods at once makes it hard to learn anything and can affect nutrition. A dietitian can guide a low-FODMAP trial [1]. See what the research says about bloating.

Do I need tests?

Most people don't need scans or scopes. Tests are for warning signs, recent worsening or an abnormal exam [1].
