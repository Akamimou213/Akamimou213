What does my poop type mean?

What's normal, how accurate the chart is, color, and when to see a doctor.

Short answer

The Bristol stool chart sorts poop into 7 types by shape. Hard pellets (types 1–2) usually mean slow transit and point toward constipation, smooth soft logs (types 3–4) are often considered ideal, and mushy or liquid stool (types 6–7) means fast transit [1]. Most people vary from day to day, so a week of logs says more than one bowel movement [3].

What's normal?

In a US national survey, 96% of adults with normal bowel habits went between three times a day and three times a week [4]. Shape varies too: in a UK study only about 56–61% of stools were the "normal" types [3]. A lasting change from your normal matters more than any single day.

Is the stool chart accurate?

Reasonably. Stool type tracked whole-gut transit time in the original study [1]. When people classified stool models, 81% were correct overall, but the borderline types (2, 3, 5 and 6) were harder [2]. More in what the research says about the stool chart.

What about color?

Brown in any shade is normal. Green, yellow or orange is usually from food. Black and tarry, red or maroon, or pale and clay-colored stool needs a doctor. See the poop color guide.

When to see a doctor

  • Blood in your stool, or black, tarry stool
  • Diarrhea lasting more than 2 days, high fever, 6 or more loose stools a day, or signs of dehydration [5]
  • Constipation with constant belly pain, vomiting, fever, not being able to pass gas, or weight loss without trying [6]
  • A change in bowel habits that lasts, especially if you're 50 or older or have a family history of bowel cancer

Questions

What's the healthiest poop type?

Types 3 and 4 are often considered ideal: formed, soft and easy to pass. Type 5 is common too, and some researchers group types 3–5 as normal [2].

What does type 6 mean?

Mushy stool with ragged edges means food moved through quickly. It's at the mild end of diarrhea. An occasional type 6 after coffee or a big meal is common; several days in a row is worth tracking. See type 6 on the chart.

Is it bad to have type 1 or 2?

Hard, lumpy stool usually means slow transit, one of the signs of constipation. Fiber added slowly, water and a regular toilet routine often help. If it lasts for weeks, talk to a doctor.
