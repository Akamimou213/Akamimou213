        What does regular actually mean?

        There's no single right number. Here's what the research says, and when to see a doctor.

        Short answer

        In a US national survey, 96% of adults with normal bowel habits went between three times a day and three times a week [1]. Soft, formed stool passed without straining is common; types 3–4 are often considered ideal, but stool shape varies from day to day even in healthy people [5]. A change from your normal matters more than matching anyone else's.

        Is this a test for IBS or constipation?

        No. It's a self-check that shows which way your answers lean. Only a clinician can diagnose IBS, chronic constipation or anything else, often after ruling out other causes. What it can do is tell you whether your pattern is worth tracking, and help you describe it clearly at an appointment.

        Why track instead of guessing?

        Memory is a poor record of bowel habits; people tend to remember the worst days. A week or two of logs, with stool type, meals and symptoms side by side, shows the real pattern. See what the research says about diaries vs memory.

        Questions

        Do I need to give my email?

        No. Your result shows straight away. Email is optional, if you want your results and the 7-day plan sent to you.

        Where do my answers go?

        Unless you ask for an email, your answers stay in your browser and aren't sent anywhere. If you do, they're stored with your email only to send your results. [CONFIRM with legal]

        Is my score a medical result?

        No. It summarizes your answers using editorial weights that haven't been validated in a study. Use it to compare yourself with yourself over time.
