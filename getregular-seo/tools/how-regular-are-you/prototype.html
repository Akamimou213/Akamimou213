      Your answers

        What does "regular" actually mean?

        Short answer: there's no single "right" number. In a US national survey, 96% of adults with normal bowel habits went between three times a day and three times a week [1]. Soft, formed stool passed without straining is common; Bristol types 3–4 are often considered ideal, but stool shape varies from day to day even in healthy people [5]. A change from your normal matters more than matching someone else's.

        Is this a test for IBS or constipation?

        No. It's a self-check that shows which way your answers lean. Only a clinician can diagnose IBS, chronic constipation or anything else, often after ruling out other causes. What the check can do is tell you whether your pattern is worth tracking, and help you describe it clearly at an appointment.

        Why track instead of guessing?

        Memory is a poor record of bowel habits: people tend to remember the worst days. A week or two of logs, with stool type, meals and symptoms side by side, shows the real pattern and makes a doctor's visit faster.
