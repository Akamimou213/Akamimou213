    What color should poop be?

    Short answer: brown, in any shade from light to dark. Green, yellow, orange or reddish poop is usually from food or dye and goes back to brown within a few days. Black and tarry, red or maroon, and pale or clay-colored stool can signal bleeding or a bile, liver or pancreas problem, and need a doctor [1].

    How to tell if it's from food

    1. Look back 1–2 days. Food usually takes a day or more to pass, so think about yesterday's meals.
    2. Check how you feel. If you feel fine and don't have diarrhea, a color change is most likely from something you ate [2].
    3. Wait and watch. Once the food has passed, poop should go back to brown. If it doesn't within a few days, contact a provider [1].

    Don't wait and watch for black and tarry, maroon, or pale stool with yellow skin, or for any visible blood.

    Frequently asked questions

    What color poop is bad?

    Black and tarry, red or maroon (possible blood), and pale, white or clay-colored stool need the most attention [1][3].

    Why is my poop black after Pepto-Bismol or iron?

    Bismuth and iron commonly turn stool black without any bleeding [3]. Stool that's sticky, tarry and smells unusually strong is different: get it checked the same day.

    How long does red poop last after beets?

    Usually until the beets have passed, often a day or two. If red continues after that, or you didn't eat red foods, contact a doctor.

    Is this for babies?

    No. Baby and infant stool colors follow different rules; ask your pediatrician.
