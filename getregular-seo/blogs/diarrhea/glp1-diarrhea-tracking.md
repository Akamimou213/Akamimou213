> **Quick answer:** Diarrhea is one of the most common side effects of GLP-1 medicines. In Zepbound's FDA label, 19–23% of people in weight-loss trials had diarrhea versus 8% on placebo; for Wegovy it was 30% versus 16%. Most episodes happened while doses were being increased and eased over time. Log your injection day, meals (especially fat and portion size), fluids and stool type, and share it with your prescriber. Don't change your dose on your own.

If your stomach has felt unpredictable since starting tirzepatide or semaglutide, you're in large company. Gut side effects are the most common reason people struggle with these medicines, and many people find that what they eat, and how much, seems to change how they feel.

This guide sticks to what the FDA labels and published guidance say. It doesn't tell you how to adjust your medicine; that's your prescriber's job. What it does give you is a clear way to record what's happening, so the conversation with your prescriber is faster and more useful.

**Key takeaways**
- Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting and constipation are all listed as common GI side effects in the Zepbound, Wegovy and Ozempic labels [1][2][3].
- The labels say most nausea, vomiting and diarrhea occurred during dose escalation and decreased over time [1][3].
- Diarrhea can dehydrate you, and the labels warn that dehydration from GI side effects can lead to kidney problems; drink fluids [1][2].
- Expert guidance suggests smaller, low-fat, easy-to-digest meals to reduce GI side effects [4].
- Severe belly pain (especially radiating to your back), yellow skin or eyes, or being unable to keep fluids down needs prompt medical attention [1].

## How common is diarrhea on Zepbound, Wegovy and Ozempic?

**Diarrhea is common on all three, and it's more common at the higher doses used for weight loss.** These figures come from each drug's FDA prescribing information and reflect placebo-controlled clinical trials.

| Medicine (active ingredient, trial population) | Diarrhea on drug | Diarrhea on placebo | Other common GI effects on drug |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zepbound (tirzepatide), weight reduction, 5 / 10 / 15 mg | 19% / 21% / 23% | 8% | Nausea 25–29%, constipation 11–17%, vomiting 8–13% [1] |
| Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4 mg), weight reduction, adults | 30% | 16% | Nausea 44%, constipation 24%, vomiting 24% [2] |
| Ozempic (semaglutide 0.5 / 1 mg), type 2 diabetes | 8.5% / 8.8% | 1.9% | Nausea 15.8–20.3%, vomiting 5–9.2%, constipation 3.1–5% [3] |

A few things to keep in mind when reading this table:

- **Trials differ.** Ozempic's figures come from different trials, doses and patients (people with type 2 diabetes), so they aren't directly comparable with the weight-loss trials [3].
- **Placebo groups had diarrhea too.** In Wegovy's trials, 16% of people on placebo reported diarrhea [2], a reminder that not every loose stool is the medicine.
- **Overall GI effects are very common.** 73% of adults on Wegovy and 47% on placebo reported some GI side effect [2]; for Zepbound it was 56% at each dose versus 30% on placebo [1].

## When does GLP-1 diarrhea happen and how long does it last?

**For most people, GI side effects cluster around starting the medicine and each dose increase, then settle.** The Zepbound label says most nausea, vomiting and diarrhea occurred during dose escalation and decreased over time [1]; the Ozempic label says the same [3], and the Wegovy label notes GI reactions increased during dose escalation [2].

An expert consensus on managing these side effects reports that diarrhea typically starts within the first four weeks of treatment, that most GI events are mild to moderate, and that diarrhea has been reported to last about three days in people with obesity [4].

### Why does diarrhea happen after eating on Wegovy or Zepbound?

**Many people notice symptoms are worse after large or high-fat meals.** The expert consensus recommends smaller portions, eating slowly, and choosing easy-to-digest, low-fat foods to minimize GI side effects [4]. If greasy meals reliably trigger loose stools for you, our guide to [diarrhea after fatty food](/blog/diarrhea-after-fatty-food) covers other reasons that can happen.

The same guidance suggests limiting dairy, coffee, alcohol, very cold or hot foods and sugar-alcohol sweeteners (sorbitol, mannitol, xylitol, maltitol) while you have diarrhea, and staying well hydrated with small sips [4]. Those are reasonable things to try and record, but check any significant diet change with your care team.

## How often should you poop on a GLP-1?

**There's no special "GLP-1 number"; what matters is your usual pattern and whether it's comfortable.** As general reference points, NIDDK describes constipation as fewer than three bowel movements a week or hard, dry, lumpy stools [7], and diarrhea as loose, watery stools three or more times a day or more often than is normal for you [5]. What changes on these medicines is that you may swing toward diarrhea, toward constipation, or between the two.

Constipation is listed as common in all three labels (for example, 17% on Zepbound 5 mg versus 5% on placebo) [1][2][3]. Eating much less can also mean less stool. If you've gone several days without a bowel movement, see our guide on [what to do if you haven't pooped in days](/blog/havent-pooped-in-days), and tell your prescriber if it's new or uncomfortable.

## What to track: a GLP-1 gut log

**Track injection day, meals, fluids and every bowel movement for at least two to four weeks, and especially around any dose change your prescriber makes.** The goal is to give your prescriber a clear picture: when symptoms start relative to your injection, which meals line up with them, and whether they're improving.

**What to record**

1. **Medicine:** injection day and time, and the dose your prescriber has you on (just record it; don't change it).
2. **Meals:** what you ate, roughly how much (small, normal, large), and whether it was high-fat or fried.
3. **Fluids:** water and other drinks across the day; note alcohol and coffee.
4. **Bowel movements:** time and Bristol type (1–2 hard, 3–4 typical, 5–7 loose).
5. **Symptoms:** nausea, vomiting, belly pain, bloating, dizziness, and how severe (mild, moderate, severe).

**Illustrative 7-day log** (example data, not a real patient; dose labels omitted on purpose)

| Day | Injection | Meals (size / fat) | Fluids | Bowel movements (Bristol) | Symptoms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Yes, 8 am | Small / low; small / low | ~6 cups | 1 × type 4 | Mild nausea evening |
| Tue | — | Normal / low; large / high (fried chicken) | ~5 cups | 3 × type 6–7 after dinner | Cramping, moderate |
| Wed | — | Small / low; small / low | ~8 cups | 2 × type 5 | Mild |
| Thu | — | Small / low; normal / medium | ~7 cups | 1 × type 4 | None |
| Fri | — | Normal / high (pizza) | ~4 cups | 2 × type 6 | Bloating, mild nausea |
| Sat | — | Small / low; small / low | ~7 cups | 1 × type 4 | None |
| Sun | — | Small / low; normal / low | ~7 cups | none | None |

**How to read it:** The worst day followed a large, fried dinner the day after the injection, and the second-worst followed another high-fat meal on a lower-fluid day. That's a pattern to bring to your prescriber, along with the reassuring trend that low-fat, smaller-meal days were mostly symptom-free.

## What can help GLP-1 diarrhea?

**Start with the measures in published guidance, and talk to your prescriber before changing anything about your medicine.** Expert consensus suggests [4]:

- **Smaller portions, eaten slowly,** and stopping when you feel full
- **Low-fat, bland, easy-to-digest foods** while symptoms are active (for example broth, rice, carrots, very ripe fruit without skin)
- **Steady fluids in small sips** through the day
- **Temporarily limiting** dairy, coffee, alcohol, fizzy drinks and sugar-alcohol sweeteners
- **Telling your clinician** if diarrhea continues despite these steps

Don't skip, delay or reduce a dose on your own, and don't start anti-diarrheal medicine without asking your prescriber or pharmacist. They can check for other causes and decide what's right for you.

## When to call your prescriber or get urgent care

**Call your prescriber if diarrhea is severe, lasts more than a few days, or keeps returning, and get urgent care for any of the warning signs below.** These come from the drug labels and from general diarrhea guidance.

Get medical help right away for:
- **Severe stomach pain that won't go away,** with or without vomiting, especially pain that spreads to your back (a possible sign of pancreatitis) [1]
- **Upper belly pain, yellowing of your skin or eyes, fever, or clay-colored stools** (possible gallbladder problems) [1]
- **Signs of dehydration:** extreme thirst, peeing much less, dark urine, dizziness or light-headedness; dehydration from GI side effects can lead to kidney problems [1][5]
- **Being unable to keep fluids down,** or frequent vomiting [5]
- **Blood in your stool, or black, tarry stool** [5]

Contact your prescriber soon if:
- Diarrhea lasts more than 2 days or you have six or more loose stools a day [5]
- Diarrhea continues weeks after your last dose change (diarrhea lasting 4 weeks or more is considered chronic) [6]
- It wakes you at night, or you have a fever
- You've recently taken antibiotics or traveled [5]

For general bowel warning signs, see [when to see a doctor about bowel changes](/blog/when-to-see-doctor-bowel-changes).

## FAQ

### Does Zepbound diarrhea go away?

For most people in trials it eased over time. The Zepbound label says most nausea, vomiting and diarrhea occurred during dose escalation and decreased over time [1]. If it doesn't settle, or it's severe, contact your prescriber.

### Is diarrhea after Ozempic a sign it's working?

No. Diarrhea is a side effect, not a measure of how well the medicine works. Many people have no diarrhea at all and still respond to treatment.

### Why do I get diarrhea right after eating on Wegovy?

Large or high-fat meals are a common pattern people report, and guidance recommends smaller, low-fat meals to reduce GI side effects [4]. Logging meal size and fat content alongside stool type is the quickest way to see whether that's true for you.

### Can I take something for GLP-1 diarrhea?

Ask your prescriber or pharmacist first. Expert guidance says other causes of diarrhea should be ruled out, and that medicine such as loperamide may be considered by a clinician if diet changes don't help [4].

### Should I eat less fiber on a GLP-1?

Expert consensus suggests temporarily reducing high-fiber foods while you have GI symptoms and gradually increasing fiber once they improve [4]. If constipation becomes the main problem, your care team may advise differently.
