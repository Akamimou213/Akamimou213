> **Quick answer:** Yellow diarrhea is most often a short-lived stomach bug that moves stool through too fast for bile to be fully absorbed, or a fatty meal. Orange-yellow foods like carrots and sweet potatoes can tint stool too. Yellow, greasy, floating diarrhea that persists can point to fat malabsorption (celiac disease, pancreas or liver and bile problems) or a *Giardia* infection and needs medical evaluation. Diarrhea of any color lasting more than a week should be checked.

You glance down and the color is not what you expected: yellow, loose and a little alarming. It's one of the most common "is this bad?" moments in gut health.

The honest answer depends on the rest of the picture: how long it has lasted, whether it's greasy or floats, whether you've been sick, travelled, or had your gallbladder out. This guide walks through the common causes, the ones that need testing, and a simple way to record color and consistency so you can show a doctor.

**Key takeaways**
- A sudden bout of yellow diarrhea that clears within a few days is rarely a concern, according to Cleveland Clinic [1].
- Common causes are stomach bugs, fatty meals, orange-yellow foods, and bile changes after gallbladder removal [1].
- Greasy, foul-smelling, floating stool that keeps coming back suggests fat isn't being absorbed (steatorrhea), which needs a medical work-up [2].
- *Giardia* causes greasy, smelly diarrhea that usually lasts 2 to 6 weeks and needs stool testing [3].
- Diarrhea of any color that lasts more than a week, or comes with yellow skin or eyes, blood, black stool, fever or weight loss, needs a doctor [1][5][6].

## What makes diarrhea yellow?

**Stool gets its brown color from bile, a digestive fluid made by the liver. When stool moves through too fast, or bile or fat aren't handled normally, the color can shift to yellow.** Cleveland Clinic explains yellow diarrhea in terms of bile and speed: infections can speed up the intestines so bile isn't fully absorbed, and extra bile with extra water loosens stool and turns it yellow [1].

So the color is a clue, not a diagnosis. On its own, a single yellow loose stool tells you very little. A pattern, with timing, texture and context, tells you much more.

## What causes yellow diarrhea?

**The most common causes are a stomach bug, fatty or orange-yellow foods, and bile changes after gallbladder removal. Less often, fat malabsorption or an infection like *Giardia* is behind it.** Here's how they group.

| Cause | What it often looks like | How worried to be |
|---|---|---|
| Stomach bug or food poisoning (viral or bacterial) | Sudden loose, yellow stool; may come with vomiting, cramps, fever. Viruses such as norovirus and rotavirus, and bacteria such as *Salmonella* and *E. coli*, can speed transit so bile isn't fully absorbed [1] | Usually settles within days; follow dehydration and red-flag guidance below |
| Fatty meal | Yellow, greasy stool after fried or very rich food; see [diarrhea after fatty food](/blog/diarrhea-after-fatty-food) [1] | Occasional is usually not a concern [2] |
| Orange-yellow foods or turmeric | Stool tinted yellow after carrots, sweet potatoes, squash or turmeric; rarely causes diarrhea unless eaten in large amounts [1] | Harmless; check your recent meals |
| After gallbladder removal | Yellow diarrhea as bile flows straight from the liver into the intestines; Cleveland Clinic says this is normal while the body readjusts [1] | Common; see a doctor if it persists |
| Celiac disease | Chronic diarrhea; NIDDK describes stools as "loose, greasy, bulky, and bad-smelling" [4] | Needs testing |
| *Giardia* infection | Smelly, greasy poop that can float, with gas, cramps and tiredness; usually starts 1 to 2 weeks after infection [3] | See a doctor; needs a stool test |
| Pancreas, liver or bile-duct problems | Greasy, pale or yellow stool as fat isn't digested properly [1][2] | Needs prompt evaluation |
| Crohn's disease, bacterial overgrowth | Listed among conditions linked to fat malabsorption [2] | Needs testing |

### A note on yellow diarrhea after food poisoning

**Yellow loose stool during or just after food poisoning is common and usually reflects fast transit.** Cleveland Clinic lists norovirus, rotavirus, *Salmonella* and *E. coli* among infections that can cause it [1]. NIDDK adds that food poisoning can come from bacteria, viruses or parasites, and that undercooked meats, seafood, produce and raw milk, eggs or cheese are higher-risk foods [5].

If it started suddenly, others who ate with you are sick, and it's improving within a few days, that fits. If it drags on for weeks, think beyond food poisoning, because a parasite like *Giardia* or a post-infection issue could be involved [3][5].

## When is yellow diarrhea greasy, and why does that matter?

**Greasy, bulky, foul-smelling, pale or floating stool is called steatorrhea and means excess fat in the stool.** Cleveland Clinic says fatty stools "tend to be looser, smellier and paler in color, like clay" and "might float," and may also be bulky, greasy, foamy and hard to flush [2].

Occasional fatty stool after a very fatty meal is usually not a concern. But if it happens again and again and you don't know why, Cleveland Clinic advises seeking medical attention, because untreated fat malabsorption causes problems over time [2]. See also our guide to [floating poop](/blog/floating-poop).

Cleveland Clinic lists conditions linked to steatorrhea in three groups [2]:

- **Pancreas** (exocrine pancreatic insufficiency): chronic pancreatitis, cystic fibrosis, pancreatic cancer, and others.
- **Liver and bile ducts:** cholestasis, cirrhosis, primary biliary cholangitis, and others.
- **Small intestine:** celiac disease, Crohn's disease, small intestinal bacterial overgrowth, giardiasis, and others.

That list is long because many different problems end up in the same place: fat isn't being digested or absorbed. It's the reason a doctor will ask about greasy, floating stool and the color together, and why a simple record of both is useful.

### *Giardia*: the one people overlook

**Giardiasis is an intestinal infection caused by a parasite spread through contaminated water, food, surfaces and person-to-person contact.** The CDC lists diarrhea (often 2 to 5 times a day), "smelly, greasy poop that can float," gas, cramps, nausea and growing tiredness as symptoms [3]. Symptoms usually start 1 to 2 weeks after infection and last 2 to 6 weeks, and some people have long-term symptoms [3].

Think of it if you've swallowed untreated water (lake, stream or pool), have limited access to safe drinking water, have been in close contact with an infected person or young children in childcare, or have a weakened immune system [3]. Providers diagnose it with stool samples and may prescribe medicine [3].

## How long does yellow diarrhea last?

**A sudden bout that clears within a few days is rarely a concern, while persistent yellow diarrhea warrants evaluation.** Cleveland Clinic advises seeing a healthcare provider for diarrhea of any color that lasts more than a week [1]. NIDDK advises adults to get care for diarrhea lasting more than 2 days, a high fever, or six or more loose stools a day, and right away for blood, black stool, severe pain or dehydration [5].

## How to track yellow diarrhea

**Record color, consistency (Bristol type), greasiness and context for each bowel movement for 1 to 2 weeks.** Color alone is a weak clue, but color plus Bristol type plus "does it float or look greasy" plus what happened before it is a much stronger story for a clinician.

**What to record**

1. **Date, time and Bristol type.** Type 6-7 is mushy or liquid; if you need a refresher, see [Bristol type 6 poop](/blog/bristol-stool-type-6).
2. **Color,** in plain words: yellow, pale, light brown, green. Our [poop color chart](/blog/poop-color-chart) can help you name it.
3. **Greasy, floating, foul-smelling or hard to flush?** Note it.
4. **Meal context.** Fatty meal, orange vegetables or turmeric, new supplement, or an unusual food source (restaurant, buffet, undercooked meat).
5. **Illness and exposure.** Vomiting, fever, others sick, recent travel, untreated water, antibiotics.
6. **Other symptoms.** Pain, weight change, dark urine, yellowing skin or eyes.

**Illustrative 6-day log** (example data, not a real person)

| Day | Meal context | Stool (Bristol, color, notes) | Other |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Takeout buffet, dinner | Type 7, yellow, urgent, 23:00 | Nausea, mild fever |
| 2 | Toast, broth | Type 7 x3, yellow | Others at the dinner also sick |
| 3 | Rice, bananas | Type 6, yellow-brown | Fever gone |
| 4 | Normal diet | Type 5, light brown | Feeling better |
| 5 | Normal diet | Type 4, brown | None |
| 6 | Normal diet | Type 4, brown | None |

**How to read it:** yellow, loose stool starting the night after a shared buffet, with nausea and fever, shifting to brown by day 5, is a pattern consistent with a short gastroenteritis episode. Compare that to a different, made-up pattern: yellow, greasy, floating Type 6 stools most days for three weeks with no obvious illness. That second shape is the one that would prompt a conversation with a doctor about malabsorption or infection, and a log that records it makes that conversation much faster. ## What can I do at home?

**For a short bout without red flags, rest, fluids and bland food are usually enough, but ongoing yellow diarrhea needs a diagnosis first.**

- **Replace fluids.** Watch for dehydration signs: extreme thirst or dry mouth, urinating less, dizziness, dark urine [5].
- **Look back at the last 48 hours.** Orange vegetables, turmeric, a very fatty meal, or a stomach bug exposure may explain it [1].
- **If your gallbladder was removed,** Cleveland Clinic notes that reducing high-fat foods can help if yellow diarrhea persists, and you should tell your doctor [1].
- **Avoid anti-diarrheal medicine** if you have fever or blood in the stool, and ask a doctor before using it for ongoing symptoms.

## When to see a doctor

**See a doctor if yellow diarrhea lasts more than a week, keeps coming back, or comes with any warning sign below.** Cleveland Clinic advises seeing a provider for diarrhea of any color lasting more than a week, and says persistent yellow diarrhea warrants medical evaluation [1].

**Get care right away for:**
- Yellowing of the skin or whites of the eyes (jaundice), especially with upper belly pain, fever or chills, nausea and vomiting, or a fast heartbeat. NIDDK lists these as signs that need urgent care in pancreatitis [6]
- Blood or pus in the stool, or black, tarry stool [5]
- Severe abdominal or rectal pain, frequent vomiting, or a change in mental state [5]
- Signs of dehydration [5]
- A high fever, or six or more loose stools a day [5]

**Book an appointment soon for:**
- Yellow diarrhea that lasts more than a week, or greasy, floating, foul-smelling stool that keeps recurring [1][2]
- Diarrhea after swallowing untreated water, in case of *Giardia* [3]
- Unintended weight loss, or diarrhea that wakes you at night
- Yellow diarrhea that began after gallbladder removal and isn't settling [1]
- A new, lasting change in bowel habit at 45 or older, the age US guidelines start routine colorectal cancer screening [7]
- A family history of celiac disease, IBD or colorectal cancer

## FAQ

### Is yellow diarrhea serious?

Often not. A sudden bout that clears within a few days is rarely a concern, according to Cleveland Clinic [1]. It deserves attention if it persists beyond a week, is greasy and floating, or comes with yellow skin or eyes, blood, black stool, fever or weight loss [1][2][5][6].

### Why is my poop yellow after food poisoning?

Infections can speed the intestines so bile isn't fully absorbed, which turns loose stool yellow [1]. Norovirus, rotavirus, *Salmonella* and *E. coli* are examples [1]. It should improve over a few days; if it doesn't, see a doctor.

### What does yellow, greasy, floating diarrhea mean?

It suggests fat in the stool (steatorrhea). Occasional fatty stool after a fatty meal is usually not a concern, but recurring greasy stool should be evaluated, because the list of possible causes includes celiac disease, pancreas and liver problems, and *Giardia* [2][3]. See also [floating poop](/blog/floating-poop).

### Is yellow diarrhea normal after gallbladder removal?

Cleveland Clinic says it's normal to have yellow diarrhea after gallbladder removal as your body readjusts to handling bile [1]. If it persists, tell your doctor; reducing high-fat foods can help, and bile-related causes can be treated [1].
