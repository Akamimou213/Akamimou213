> **Quick answer:** Coffee makes many people poop because it stimulates the colon within minutes. Caffeine is part of it, but coffee also triggers gut hormones such as gastrin, which is why decaf can have a milder version of the same effect. Studies suggest roughly 3 in 10 people feel a strong urge to go after a cup. Loose stools usually come from strong coffee, an empty stomach, milk (lactose), sweeteners, or a sensitive gut such as IBS.

The morning coffee-then-bathroom routine is so common it has a nickname: the "coffee poop". For many people it's a welcome part of being regular. For others it's an urgent, loose dash that makes the commute stressful.

The useful question isn't just "why", it's "which part of my coffee is doing it". Caffeine, the coffee itself, the milk, the sweetener and the timing all play a role, and you can test each one.

**Key takeaways**
- About 29% of people in one study of 99 healthy volunteers felt a compelling need to poop after a cup of coffee [1].
- Caffeinated coffee stimulated the colon about as much as a 1,000-calorie meal and 60% more than water in a small study [2].
- Decaf still has a laxative effect, just a weaker one [3].
- Milk, cream and sugar-free sweeteners are common hidden culprits when coffee causes loose stools [3][5][6].
- Coffee shouldn't cause watery diarrhea every day. If it seems to, test the add-ins and talk to a doctor if it continues.

## Why does coffee make you poop?

**Coffee makes you poop because it switches on colon contractions quickly, through caffeine and through gut hormones released by the coffee itself.** In a study of 99 healthy volunteers, 29% described a compelling need to defecate after a cup of coffee, and colon activity rose within minutes [1]. Cleveland Clinic notes the urge can come within about four minutes for some people [3].

Here's what seems to be going on:

- **Caffeine.** In a small manometry study of 12 healthy adults, caffeinated coffee increased colon activity about as much as a 1,000-calorie meal, 60% more than water and 23% more than decaf [2].
- **Gastrin.** Coffee prompts the stomach to release gastrin, a hormone that increases contractions that move stool along [3]. A review notes coffee raised blood gastrin levels about 2.3-fold in one study [1].
- **Cholecystokinin (CCK).** There's also evidence coffee increases CCK, another digestive hormone [3].
- **Timing.** Your colon is already about three times more active after waking than during sleep [4], and a warm drink with breakfast adds the gastrocolic reflex on top [3].

That's why a morning cup works so reliably. If your urgency is mainly a morning problem, our guide to [urgent bowel movements in the morning](/blog/urgent-bowel-movement-morning) explains the other pieces.

### What are coffee poops?

**"Coffee poop" is the casual name for the bowel movement that follows soon after drinking coffee.** It's usually a normal, formed stool (Bristol type 3–5) that was already waiting in your colon; coffee just speeds its exit. It isn't the coffee itself coming straight through.

## Is it normal for coffee to make you poop?

**Yes, it's normal, and it's not a sign that anything is wrong.** Only a minority of people notice the effect strongly [1], and Cleveland Clinic notes that regular drinkers may become less sensitive over time [3].

It's less normal if coffee reliably causes:
- Watery stools (Bristol type 6–7)
- Several trips in a row
- Cramping that's more than mild
- Urgency you can't control

## Why does coffee give me diarrhea?

**Coffee causes diarrhea when its stimulating effect is stronger than usual, or when something added to it is the real trigger.** Common reasons:

| Possible reason | Why it can loosen stools | Clue in your pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Strong or large coffee | More caffeine, bigger colon response [2] | Worse with espresso shots, cold brew or big sizes |
| Empty stomach | Nothing to buffer the stimulus | Worse when coffee replaces breakfast |
| Milk or cream | Lactose can cause loose stools 30 minutes to 2 hours later in people with low lactase [5]; Cleveland Clinic notes fats and sugars also affect transit [3] | Black coffee is fine, lattes aren't |
| Sugar-free sweeteners | Sugar alcohols (sorbitol, mannitol, xylitol) can cause diarrhea [6] | Worse with sugar-free syrups or flavored creamers |
| IBS or a sensitive gut | A stronger gut response to normal stimuli [3] | Coffee is one of several triggers |
| Too much caffeine overall | The FDA cites about 400 mg a day (roughly two to three 12-oz cups) as an amount not generally linked to negative effects in most adults [7] | Several coffees plus energy drinks or tea |

Coffee can also cause bloating and gas in some people, a separate topic covered in [can coffee cause bloating](/blog/can-coffee-cause-bloating). If milk seems to be the problem, see [diarrhea after dairy](/blog/diarrhea-after-dairy).

## How to find out what in your coffee is the trigger

**Change one variable at a time for 3–4 days each, and log what comes out.** Coffee has at least four suspects (caffeine, the coffee itself, milk, sweetener), so changing everything at once tells you nothing.

**A simple 2-week coffee test**

| Days | What you drink | What it tests |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Your usual coffee, logged carefully | Your baseline |
| 4–6 | Same coffee, black, no sweetener | Whether milk/creamer/sweetener matters |
| 7–9 | Black decaf | Whether caffeine matters |
| 10–12 | Your usual coffee, but with breakfast | Whether an empty stomach matters |
| 13–14 | Back to usual | Whether symptoms return |

**Illustrative result** (example data, not a real patient)

| Phase | Avg. time to first BM | Typical Bristol type | Loose stools (type 6–7) per day |
|---|---|---|---|
| Usual oat-milk latte + vanilla syrup (sugar-free) | 15 min | 6 | 2 |
| Black, no syrup | 20 min | 4 | 0 |
| Black decaf | 35 min | 4 | 0 |
| Usual latte with breakfast | 25 min | 6 | 1–2 |
| Back to usual | 15 min | 6 | 2 |

**How to read it:** The urge to go stayed (that's the normal coffee effect), but loose stools disappeared when the syrup and milk did and came back when they returned. Sugar-free syrup is the first suspect here; the next step would be a latte without the syrup.

**What to log each day:** coffee time, size, type (regular or decaf), add-ins, whether you'd eaten, then every bowel movement's time and Bristol type. ## How can I stop coffee from giving me diarrhea?

**Start with the change your test points to.** General options:

- **Drink it with food** rather than on an empty stomach.
- **Go smaller or weaker,** or swap one cup for decaf.
- **Try lactose-free milk** or black coffee if dairy seems involved [5].
- **Check syrups and creamers** for sorbitol, mannitol, xylitol or other sugar alcohols [6].
- **Spread caffeine out** and keep your daily total in mind [7].

If you depend on coffee to poop at all, that's worth mentioning to a doctor too; it can be a sign of underlying constipation.

## When to see a doctor

**Coffee should not cause daily watery diarrhea, blood, or pain.** See a doctor if loose stools continue after you've tested coffee and add-ins, or if you notice:

- Blood or pus in your stool, or black, tarry stool [6]
- Fever, severe pain in your abdomen or rectum, or frequent vomiting [6]
- Signs of dehydration, such as extreme thirst, dark urine or dizziness [6]
- Diarrhea that wakes you at night, or unintended weight loss
- Diarrhea lasting 4 weeks or more, which is considered chronic [8]
- Diarrhea after antibiotics or recent travel [6]

## FAQ

### Why does coffee make me poop but energy drinks don't?

Coffee seems to stimulate the gut through more than caffeine, including gastrin and other compounds in the coffee itself [3]. Cleveland Clinic notes that caffeinated energy drinks don't have the same effect, which suggests caffeine alone doesn't explain it [3].

### Does decaf make you poop?

It can. Decaf contains the same acids and still has a laxative effect, just weaker than regular coffee [3]. In one small study, regular coffee stimulated the colon 23% more than decaf [2].

### How long after coffee do you poop?

Often within minutes. Colon activity can rise within minutes of a cup [1], and Cleveland Clinic reports urges within about four minutes for some people [3]. If milk is the trigger, loose stools may come 30 minutes to 2 hours later instead [5].

### Is it bad to rely on coffee to poop?

Not necessarily, but if you can't go without it, it may point to constipation that's worth addressing with fiber, fluids, movement and, if needed, a doctor's advice.
