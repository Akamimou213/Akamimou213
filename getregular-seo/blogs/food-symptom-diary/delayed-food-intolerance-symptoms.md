> **Quick answer:** Food intolerance symptoms usually start a few hours after eating the problem food, and they can last a few hours or a few days. Lactose symptoms often appear within a few hours. FODMAP-related bloating and gas usually take at least about 4 hours, because the food has to reach the large intestine first. Symptoms within minutes of eating are usually the gut's normal reflex to a meal, not the food you just ate.

If you feel bloated at 4 p.m., it's natural to blame lunch. But lunch may be innocent. The culprit could be breakfast, last night's dinner, or a combination of both. This timing gap is the single biggest reason people struggle to find their trigger foods.

This guide explains why symptoms are delayed, the typical windows for common causes, and a simple look-back method so "yesterday's lunch" gets the attention it deserves.

**Key takeaways**
- Intolerance symptoms usually start a few hours after eating and can last hours to days [1].
- FODMAP symptoms usually take at least about 4 hours, because they act mainly in the lower gut [3].
- Symptoms within minutes of eating are often the gastrocolic reflex, not a reaction to that meal [3].
- When you get a symptom, look back at least 24 hours, not just at the last meal.
- A few delayed reactions are allergies, not intolerances. Alpha-gal syndrome (a meat allergy linked to tick bites) typically starts 2–6 hours after eating [5].

## How long after eating do food intolerance symptoms start?

Most food intolerance symptoms start a few hours after eating, according to the NHS [1]. The exact window depends on what's causing the reaction and where in the gut it happens. Here's a timing map of the most common patterns:

| Time after eating | What's often going on | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Minutes to an hour | The gastrocolic reflex: eating triggers bowel contractions, which can be exaggerated in IBS and stronger after very large or fatty meals [3] | Urgent need to poop during or right after a meal |
| Within a few hours | Lactose intolerance: the small intestine doesn't make enough lactase to digest the lactose you had [2] | Gas, bloating, cramps or diarrhea after a milkshake |
| About 4 hours or more | FODMAP fermentation in the lower gut [3] | Afternoon bloating after a lunch with onion, garlic or beans |
| Evening or next morning | Effects of earlier meals reaching the colon; looser or more urgent stools | Type 6 stool the morning after a large, high-FODMAP dinner |
| 2–6 hours | Alpha-gal syndrome, an *allergy* to a sugar in red meat and some dairy products, linked to tick bites [5] | Hives, stomach pain or diarrhea in the evening after a burger at lunch |
| Within minutes to about 2 hours, with hives, swelling or wheeze | Typical food *allergy* | Needs medical care, not a diary |

Use these as rough guides, not rules. Digestion speeds vary a lot from person to person. In capsule studies, normal small-bowel transit ranged from 2 to 6 hours and whole-gut transit from 10 to 73 hours [4]. So one person's "4 hours" is another person's "8 hours."

## Why are food intolerance symptoms delayed?

Symptoms are delayed because most intolerance reactions happen once food reaches the small intestine or colon, not in the stomach. In wireless capsule studies, normal ranges are about 2–5 hours to leave the stomach and another 2–6 hours to pass through the small intestine [4]. FODMAPs mostly act in the small and large intestine, which is why Monash dietitians say FODMAP symptoms usually take at least about 4 hours to appear [3]. Lactose intolerance happens when the small intestine makes less lactase, the enzyme that digests milk sugar [2], so symptoms build as the meal moves through.

That's also why "I ate it and felt bad immediately" usually isn't a reaction to the food you just ate. Monash notes that the immediate symptoms many people with IBS feel are clearly not caused by FODMAPs in that meal [3]. They're more often the gastrocolic reflex, sometimes reacting to food eaten hours earlier that's now further down.

Dose and stacking add another layer. A small amount of a problem food might cause nothing, while a larger amount, or several FODMAP-containing foods in one sitting, adds up and tips you into symptoms [7]. NIDDK notes that lactose symptoms can be mild or severe depending on how much you have [2].

## How long do food intolerance symptoms last?

Food intolerance symptoms can last a few hours or a few days [1]. Bloating and gas often settle once the food has moved through. Changes in stool can show up the next day. Because effects can linger, Monash's FODMAP reintroduction guidance recommends a break of 2–3 days between food challenges, or until symptoms settle, so one test doesn't blur into the next [6].

How long a reaction lasts can also be a clue in your diary. If you're still bloated two days after one meal, it's worth checking whether something else (stress, your cycle, a new medicine, ongoing meals with the same ingredient) is keeping it going. And if a food that never bothered you now does, see [can you suddenly develop a food intolerance?](/blog/can-food-intolerance-start-suddenly).

## How to trace a delayed reaction

To trace a delayed reaction, start from the symptom's start time and look back through every meal and drink in the previous 24 hours, then check the day before that. Here's the method with an illustrative example.

**Illustrative example (not a real case):** Bloating 6/10 starts Wednesday at 10:00 a.m., with a type 6 stool at 8:00 a.m.

| Look-back window | Meals in that window | Suspects |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 h before (7:00–10:00 Wed) | Coffee, toast | Unlikely to explain bloating this quickly; more likely the reflex [3] |
| 3–12 h before (22:00 Tue–7:00 Wed) | Ice cream at 21:30 Tue | Lactose; within a plausible window [2] |
| 12–24 h before (10:00–22:00 Tue) | Lunch: lentil soup, garlic bread; dinner: pasta with onion and cream sauce | Fructans and GOS (onion, garlic, wheat, lentils) and lactose (cream), several in one day, a possible stacking effect [7] |
| 24–48 h before (Mon–Tue morning) | Normal meals, no obvious suspects | Lower priority |

What this shows: the breakfast that "caused" the bloating is the least likely suspect. Tuesday, with several suspect foods and dairy, is the day to look at. One episode proves nothing; repeat the look-back for every symptom over two weeks and see which suspects keep turning up.

Three habits make delayed reactions much easier to catch:
1. **Log the time of every meal, drink and snack.** Without times, a look-back is guesswork.
2. **Log the time a symptom *started*,** not when it peaked.
3. **Log bowel movements with their Bristol type.** Stool form is a rough guide to transit speed [8], and next-day changes often point back to the day before.

## When to see a doctor

Delayed digestive symptoms are common and often harmless, but see a doctor if you have [9]:
- Blood in your stool, or black, tarry stools
- Unintended weight loss or anemia
- Symptoms that wake you at night, persistent fever or severe pain
- Ongoing diarrhea or a lasting change in bowel habits, especially if new after age 50
- A family history of celiac disease, inflammatory bowel disease or colon cancer

Get urgent help for hives, swelling of the lips, tongue or throat, wheezing, trouble breathing or dizziness after eating, even hours later. Those suggest an allergic reaction. If you've had tick bites and get reactions a few hours after eating red meat, ask your doctor about alpha-gal syndrome [5].

## FAQ

**Can food intolerance symptoms show up the next day?**
Yes. The NHS notes symptoms can last a few hours or days [1], and stool changes in particular often appear the morning after. That's why a look-back of at least 24 hours is useful.

**How long after eating dairy do symptoms start?**
With lactose intolerance, symptoms often start within a few hours of having milk or milk products, and their severity depends on how much lactose you had [2].

**Why do I get symptoms right after eating?**
Usually the gastrocolic reflex, the normal signal that tells your bowel to move when your stomach fills. It can be exaggerated in IBS and with very large or fatty meals [3]. It doesn't mean the food you just ate is a trigger.

**Can a food intolerance reaction take 72 hours?**
Whole-gut transit can range up to about 73 hours in healthy people [4], so effects from a meal can, in principle, show up days later. In practice, most diary detective work focuses on the previous 24–48 hours, and longer windows make it harder to tell signal from noise.

**Are delayed reactions a sign of an IgG food sensitivity?**
No. Delayed symptoms are explained by digestion time and dose. IgG food tests aren't recommended for diagnosing intolerance; the NHS says tests you can buy for this have limited evidence of accuracy [1].
