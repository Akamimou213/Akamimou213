## What do free tiers usually limit?

Free tiers usually limit analysis, backup, photos or reports, not the basic act of logging. Plop's listing is a clear example: logging, history, charts and CSV export are free, while custom categories, photos, advanced analysis, monthly PDF reports and cloud backup are premium [1].

Common limits to look for:
- **History.** Can you still see last month's entries? Some reviewers of other trackers describe locked history or limited date ranges behind a paywall. The listing's free-tier text is the best predictor.
- **Export.** Free CSV is a useful signal that your data isn't trapped. PDF reports are often paid.
- **Analysis.** "Basic analysis" is free; machine-learning or AI summaries are often premium.
- **Backup and sync.** On-device storage is free but doesn't survive a lost phone unless you back up. Cloud sync is often premium. Plop, for example, stores data locally by default and charges for cloud backup [1].
- **Ads.** Check reviews for ads after each log.

> **Original asset: the free-tier test.** On day one, in any "free" app: (1) log three entries; (2) open a chart; (3) try to export; (4) read the listing's "In-App Purchases" section and any in-app upgrade screen to see what's locked. If anything you'd need for a doctor's visit is behind a paywall, decide now, not in week three.

## Are there free options that aren't apps?

Yes, and for a clinician visit they work well. A paper or spreadsheet diary has no paywall, no privacy label and no export button to fail.

- **Printable diaries.** Use the [food and symptom diary](/resources/food-symptom-diary) and the [Bristol stool chart](/resources/bristol-stool-chart) as a reference for type numbers.
- **Apple Health symptoms.** Apple's developer documentation lists constipation, diarrhea, bloating and abdominal cramps among its symptom types, but we found no stool-form log ([details](/blog/apple-health-poop-tracking)).
- **A notes app.** A one-line-per-entry note ("Tue 8:15, type 6, urgent") is enough for two to four weeks.

NHS guidance notes that you may be asked to keep a food and symptoms diary to work out possible triggers, and that symptoms of food intolerance usually start a few hours after eating [17]. Any of the formats above can do that job.

## How to track your gut for free

The method matters more than the tool. Whatever you use, do these five things.

1. **Log each meal and drink when you have it**, including sauces, garlic, onion and dairy.
2. **Log each bowel movement with a Bristol type**, plus "no movement" days. See [how to track bowel movements](/blog/how-to-track-bowel-movements).
3. **Log symptoms with their own time and a 0 to 10 severity.**
4. **Add context:** stress, sleep, period, travel, new medication.
5. **Keep going for two to four weeks** before reading patterns, and bring the log to your clinician.

## When to see a doctor

A free tracker can organise what you notice, but it can't rule anything out. NIDDK lists bloody or black, tarry stools, rectal bleeding, weight loss and anemia as symptoms that may suggest a problem other than IBS [18]. See a doctor promptly if you have:

- Blood in your stool, or black, tarry stool
- Unintended weight loss, or anemia
- Severe or worsening abdominal pain, fever, or symptoms that wake you at night
- A new change in bowel habits that persists, especially after age 50 or with a family history of colorectal cancer, inflammatory bowel disease or celiac disease
