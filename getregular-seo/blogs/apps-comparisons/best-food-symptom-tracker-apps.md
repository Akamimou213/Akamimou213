**Key takeaways**
- Look for four things: low-effort meal logging, symptoms logged separately with a start time, a stated analysis method, and a working export.
- Food intolerance symptoms usually start a few hours after eating and can last hours or days [1], so an app that only checks "the last meal" will miss many patterns.
- AI photo logging is good at naming foods but weaker at portion sizes and nutrient amounts [2][3]. Always edit what it guesses.
- Foods you eat every day will show up before most symptoms. That's not evidence they cause them.

## What makes a good food and symptom tracker?

A good food and symptom tracker makes logging fast enough to keep doing, keeps symptoms separate from meals, and shows you its evidence instead of just a verdict.

| Criterion | Why it matters | What to check on the listing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Logging effort per meal** | Gaps ruin pattern-spotting | Photo, quick re-log, saved meals, barcode |
| **Symptoms logged independently** | Symptoms don't only happen after meals | Can you log a symptom with its own time and severity? |
| **Time windows** | Reactions can be delayed by hours [1] | Does the analysis look beyond the previous meal? |
| **Transparent analysis** | You need to know *why* a food is flagged | Counts of times eaten vs times followed by symptoms |
| **Export** | The point is often a doctor or dietitian visit | PDF and/or CSV, and is it free? |
| **Platforms and price** | Switching apps later means losing history | Android, web, subscription vs one-time |

## Typing, barcodes or photos: which logging method is best?

The best logging method is the one with the lowest effort that still captures hidden ingredients. Each method trades speed for detail.

| Method | Speed | Captures | Misses | Apps |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Typed / database search** | Slow | Exact ingredients and brands | Nothing, if you keep it up, but many people don't | mySymptoms, CareClinic |
| **Barcode scan** | Fast for packaged food | Packaged ingredient lists | Home cooking, restaurants; reviewers report missing items | mySymptoms |
| **Free text / saved meals** | Fast for repeats | Whatever you write | Detail you skip | Bowelle, Bearable |
| **Photo + AI** | Fastest | What's visible on the plate | Sauces, oils, garlic, onion, how much you ate | FoodTrigger AI, Get Regular, Balloon |

The research supports photo logging for *identifying* foods but not for precise amounts. In one 2025 study, ChatGPT-4 correctly identified foods in 93% of meal photos but was poor at estimating medium and large portions and most nutrient amounts [2]. A 2024 scoping review of AI food-image systems found most were validated for energy and macronutrients and only a few for micronutrients [3]. For gut tracking, that's often fine; you care more about *what* you ate than its exact gram weight. Just add the invisible ingredients by hand ([photo food diary tips](/blog/photo-food-diary)).

## Why trigger analysis flags the wrong foods

Trigger analysis flags the wrong foods when it counts how often a food appears before a symptom without asking how often you eat it at all. Staples like coffee, bread or water will "precede" almost every symptom.

> **Original asset: an illustrative 5-day log (made-up data) showing the staple-food trap**
>
> | Day | Breakfast | Lunch | Dinner | Bloating (0–10), time |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | Mon | Coffee, toast | Chicken salad | Pasta with garlic sauce | 6 at 9 pm |
> | Tue | Coffee, toast | Leftover pasta | Rice, salmon | 5 at 2 pm |
> | Wed | Coffee, oats | Turkey sandwich | Stir-fry, no onion | 1 |
> | Thu | Coffee, toast | Bean soup | Pizza | 7 at 3 pm, 6 at 10 pm |
> | Fri | Coffee, oats | Chicken salad | Rice, salmon | 0 |
>
> A naive count says **coffee** preceded every bloating episode (3 of 3). But coffee was also present on the two calm days, so it doesn't separate good days from bad. Garlic/onion-heavy or bean meals (Mon dinner, Tue lunch, Thu lunch and dinner) line up with every episode and are absent on both calm days. That's a *possible* pattern worth testing with a clinician, not a conclusion.

When you assess any app's analysis, ask:
1. **Does it compare bad days with good days**, not just list what came before symptoms?
2. **Does it show counts** (eaten 9 times, followed by symptoms 7 times)?
3. **Does it use a time window of hours**, not just the previous meal? Intolerance symptoms usually start a few hours after eating and can last hours or days [1] ([more on delayed reactions](/blog/delayed-food-intolerance-symptoms)).
4. **Does it call results possible patterns** and suggest a test, rather than declaring a trigger?

For the full method, see [how to identify food triggers](/blog/how-to-identify-food-triggers).

## How to track food and symptoms (in any app)

1. Log each meal and drink when you have it, with a photo or a short note. Add hidden ingredients.
2. Log symptoms separately, with a start time and a 0–10 severity.
3. Log bowel movements with a Bristol type, plus stress, sleep and period days.
4. Keep it up for 2–4 weeks before reading patterns, then compare your worst days with your best days.
5. Test one suspect at a time, ideally with a dietitian.

## When to see a doctor

Tracking is for working out patterns, not for managing dangerous reactions. Get emergency help if food causes swelling of the lips, face or throat, wheezing or trouble breathing; those are signs of an allergic reaction, not an intolerance [11]. See a doctor soon for blood in your stool, black or tarry stool, unintended weight loss, anemia [12], symptoms that wake you at night, fever, or a lasting change in bowel habits, especially after age 50.

## FAQ

**What is the best free food and symptom tracker app?**
Bowelle is a free download with optional purchases, and Bearable has a free version. mySymptoms, FoodTrigger AI and Get Regular are subscription-based (mySymptoms and FoodTrigger AI advertise 7-day trials). Check the listing's in-app purchase section before logging weeks of data.

**Can an app tell me which foods I'm sensitive to?**
An app can show foods that tend to appear before your symptoms. It can't confirm a sensitivity or intolerance. That usually takes a structured elimination and reintroduction with a clinician, and some conditions need tests.

**How long should I track before patterns show up?**
Most people need at least 2–4 weeks of consistent logging, longer if symptoms are infrequent. Because reactions can be delayed by hours [1], one or two episodes are rarely enough.

**Is a photo food diary accurate enough?**
Photos are good for recording what you ate and when. AI estimates of portions and nutrients can be off, especially for larger meals [2], so add sauces, oils and hidden ingredients yourself.
