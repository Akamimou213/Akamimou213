## What makes a good poop tracker app?

A good poop tracker records the form and timing of each bowel movement quickly enough that you keep doing it, and gets that data back out to a clinician. Everything else is a bonus.

| Criterion | Why it matters | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bristol type** | The Bristol Stool Form Scale is the common shorthand for stool form; the original paper presents it as a useful guide to transit time [1] | Is the 1 to 7 scale used, with pictures or descriptions? |
| **Stool detail** | Clinicians ask about urgency, straining, blood, mucus and color | Are there fields beyond type, such as blood, mucus, urgency, pain? |
| **No-movement days** | A gap is data, not a missed entry | Can you log "didn't go"? |
| **Symptoms and food** | Context is what turns a stool log into a pattern | Separate symptom entries; food logging if you need it |
| **Export** | The log is only useful if your doctor sees it | PDF and/or CSV; is it free? Test it |
| **Price and free tier** | Many apps lock history or export | In-app purchase list and what the free version includes |
| **Privacy** | Stool data is sensitive | Where data is stored; what the App Privacy label says |
| **Platforms and updates** | You may switch phones | Android version; last update date |

For the method itself, see [how to track bowel movements](/blog/how-to-track-bowel-movements) and the [Bristol stool chart](/resources/bristol-stool-chart).

> **Original asset: the 30-minute poop-tracker test.** Before you commit to any app, spend one day on it:
> 1. Log one stool in under 30 seconds, including a Bristol type.
> 2. Log a "no bowel movement" day, if the app allows it.
> 3. Add a symptom with its own time.
> 4. Export a report, open it, and check that the Bristol types, dates and symptoms appear.
> 5. Find the price. Open the listing's "In-App Purchases" and the app's settings, and look for a trial.
> 6. Read the privacy label, then the privacy policy's first page.
>
> If steps 1 to 4 aren't painless, you won't log for the two to four weeks it takes to see a pattern.

## Which poop apps are for fun, not for health tracking?

Several top results for "poop tracker" are games or social toys. They're fine as novelty, but they aren't built for health tracking.

- **Poop Map** is categorised as Entertainment and Social Networking, and logs the *places* you've pooped [13]. It has a very large rating count (64,110) because it's popular, not because it records stool.
- **UNCHIKUN** is a one-tap counting app with avatars, "Poop Buddies" and streaks [13]; its listing describes recording counts, not stool form.
- **Poop Analyzer** (2011) states in its listing that it's "intended for entertainment purposes only and does not provide true stool analyzer functionality" [13].

If you want an app that helps a clinician, pick one that logs Bristol type, symptoms and timing, and that exports.

## How to track your poop with an app (whichever you choose)

Good tracking is the same in every app: log each movement soon after it happens, use the Bristol type, log the days you don't go, and add symptoms separately.

1. **Log each movement within a few hours**, with a Bristol type rather than adjectives.
2. **Log "no bowel movement" days.** A three-day gap is as informative as a type 6.
3. **Add blood, mucus, urgency or pain** if the app has fields for them.
4. **Note the context:** travel, new medication, a period, a bad night's sleep, a big change in diet.
5. **Keep going for two to four weeks** before reading patterns, then bring the report, not your conclusions, to your clinician ([how to share a log with a doctor](/blog/share-food-symptom-log-with-doctor)).

## When to see a doctor

A tracker can organise what you notice, but it can't tell you what's causing it or rule anything out. NIDDK lists bloody or black, tarry stools, rectal bleeding, weight loss and anemia as symptoms that may suggest a problem other than IBS [15]. See a doctor promptly if you have:

- Blood in your stool, or black, tarry stool
- Unintended weight loss, or anemia
- Severe or worsening abdominal pain, fever, or symptoms that wake you at night
- A new change in bowel habits that persists, especially after age 50 or with a family history of colorectal cancer, inflammatory bowel disease or celiac disease

## FAQ

**What is the best poop tracker app?**
It depends on what you need. Plop suits a stool-first log with a stated free tier, Happy Poop suits a free, simple diary, Bowelle suits quick no-account logging, Balloon suits fibre tracking and habit coaching, CareClinic's Poop Tracker suits detailed stool fields, and Get Regular suits photo-first food, stool and symptom tracking on iPhone. Check the listing's price, export and platforms first.

**What poop tracker app does Reddit recommend?**
Reddit threads mix IBS, IBD and general gut tracking, so recommendations depend on the poster's condition and phone. Treat any thread as one person's experience: match the app to what you need to log, then check the current price, export format and Android availability on the listing yourself.

**What is the best free bowel movement tracker?**
Plop states that its free tier includes unlimited logs, charts, CSV export and Apple Health integration, and Happy Poop lists only donation purchases. Bowelle is free to download with optional purchases [2][4][7]. For what "free" really includes across gut apps, see [free gut health apps](/blog/free-gut-health-apps).

**Can a poop tracker app diagnose a condition?**
No. These apps record and summarise what you log. A diagnosis comes from a clinician who reviews your symptoms and history and may order tests [15].

**Should I use photos in a poop tracker?**
Only if you're comfortable with where the photo goes. Some apps store photos on your device; others send them to servers or AI services for analysis. Check the privacy policy first, and use manual Bristol entry if you'd rather not upload images.
