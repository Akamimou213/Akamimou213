## What kinds of IBS apps are there?

IBS apps fall into three groups: trackers, therapy programs and FODMAP guides. They solve different problems, which is why comparing them on one list is usually misleading.

| Type | What it does | Examples | Evidence base |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Tracker / diary** | Logs meals, bowel movements (often on the Bristol Stool Scale), symptoms, stress and sleep, then charts or correlates them | mySymptoms, Bowelle, Bearable, CareClinic, Plop, Balloon, Get Regular | Diaries support the diet changes clinicians recommend [2]; the analysis features themselves are rarely validated |
| **Therapy program** | Delivers a structured treatment, usually gut-directed hypnotherapy or CBT, in daily sessions | Nerva; Cara Care in Germany (prescription) | ACG suggests gut-directed psychotherapy for global IBS symptoms [1]; NIDDK lists CBT and gut-directed hypnotherapy as IBS therapies [2] |
| **FODMAP guide** | Food lists with FODMAP ratings, recipes, sometimes a diary for the reintroduction phase | Monash FODMAP Diet, Fast FODMAP | ACG recommends a limited trial of a low-FODMAP diet [1]; Monash describes a 3-step diet (restrict for 2–6 weeks, reintroduce, personalise) [3] |

A **tracker** is an app whose main job is recording what you eat and how your gut responds. A **therapy program** is an app that delivers a treatment. If your doctor has diagnosed IBS and suggested hypnotherapy, a tracker isn't a substitute. If you're still figuring out your pattern, a therapy app won't give you a timeline to show your doctor.

> **Original asset: the "two-app stack."** Many people with IBS end up with one *treatment or reference* app and one *tracker*. A sensible stack: Monash (what to eat during elimination) + a tracker (what happened when you ate it). Or Nerva (daily sessions) + a tracker (did flares get less frequent?). Pick the tracker you'll actually open every day. Consistency matters more than features.

## What real users complain about in IBS apps

App Store reviews across IBS trackers point to the same handful of problems. We read reviews of mySymptoms, Cara Care, Bowelle, Balloon, Plop, Monash and Nerva; these themes came up repeatedly.

- **Surprise paywalls.** Several reviewers describe finishing sign-up and only then discovering a trial or subscription. Check the "In-App Purchases" section of the listing before you start logging.
- **"Trigger" analysis that flags everything.** Reviewers of correlation-based diaries describe being told water or nearly every food is a trigger. Foods you eat daily will always appear before symptoms; that doesn't make them the cause.
- **Exports that fail at the worst moment.** Several reviews describe email or export features not working days before a doctor's appointment. Do a test export in week one.
- **Data loss.** Some reviewers describe losing months or years of entries after an app change. Ask whether your data is backed up and whether you can export it.
- **Typing fatigue.** Logging every ingredient is the most common reason people quit, which is why photo and one-tap logging matter.

## How to track IBS with an app (whichever you pick)

Good IBS tracking is the same in every app: same-day logging, a timestamp on every symptom, and enough weeks of data to see a pattern rather than a coincidence.

1. **Log meals when you eat**, with a photo or a few words. Note the hidden stuff: sauces, garlic, onion, dairy.
2. **Log every bowel movement** with a Bristol type (1–7), and log "no movement" days too.
3. **Log symptoms with a start time and severity** (0–10), separately from meals.
4. **Add context:** stress, sleep, period, new medication.
5. **Keep going for at least 2–4 weeks** before trusting any pattern, and treat patterns as hypotheses to test with your clinician.

For a flare-by-flare method, see [how to track an IBS flare](/blog/ibs-flare-up-tracking).

## When to see a doctor

An app can organise your symptoms; it can't rule out other conditions. IBS is diagnosed by a clinician based on your symptom pattern, history and sometimes tests [4] ([how IBS is diagnosed](/blog/how-is-ibs-diagnosed)). See a doctor promptly if you have:

- Blood in your stool, or black, tarry stool [4]
- Unintended weight loss [4]
- Anemia [4]
- A family history of colorectal cancer, inflammatory bowel disease or celiac disease [4]
- Symptoms that wake you at night, fever, or severe or worsening pain
- New or changed bowel habits that start after age 50, or any change that persists

## FAQ

**What is the best app for tracking IBS symptoms?**
For pure tracking, choose based on how you like to log. mySymptoms suits detailed typists, Bowelle and Plop suit quick, free-to-start logging, Bearable suits people tracking several conditions, and Get Regular suits iPhone users who prefer photos. Check each listing's price and export options first.

**What IBS app does Reddit recommend?**
On r/IBS, people most often mention mySymptoms, Bowelle, Monash and Nerva, and recommendations vary with what each person needs. Treat any single thread as one person's experience and check the listing details yourself.

**Is there a free IBS app?**
Yes. Bowelle and Fast FODMAP are free downloads, and Plop and Bearable have free versions. Monash costs $7.99 once (US). Most AI or program-based apps are subscription-based; see [free gut health apps](/blog/free-gut-health-apps) for what free tiers include.

**Can an app diagnose IBS?**
No. Apps in this list, including Get Regular, are tracking or wellness tools. A diagnosis comes from a clinician who reviews your symptoms and history and may order tests to rule out other conditions [4].

**Is Cara Care still available in the US?**
As of 19 Oct 2026, a "Cara Care: IBS, FODMAP Tracker" listing still appears on the US App Store, but it was last updated on 27 Oct 2020 [10]. The developer's English website now describes a prescription IBS therapy app covered by health insurance in Germany [11].
