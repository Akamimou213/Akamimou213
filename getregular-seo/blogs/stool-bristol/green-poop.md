> **Quick answer:** A green bowel movement is usually harmless. The most common causes are green vegetables like spinach or kale, green, blue or purple food dye, and stool moving through your gut faster than usual, which leaves bile pigments less time to turn brown. Antibiotics and some infections can also do it. If you feel fine, it should go back to brown within a day or two. Green diarrhea with fever, pain or blood needs a doctor.

**Key takeaways**
- Food is the most likely explanation for green poop, especially leafy greens [1].
- Food- and dye-related green usually fades within a day or two once it has passed through [1].
- Fast transit, as with diarrhea, can leave stool green because bile has less time to change color [1][2].
- Antibiotics, infections (such as *Salmonella*, norovirus or *Giardia*) and conditions like IBS or IBD are less common causes [1].
- "If you feel perfectly fine and don't have diarrhea, a different color bowel movement is most likely linked to something you ate," says a Cleveland Clinic gastroenterologist [1].

## Why is my bowel movement green?

Your stool is normally brown because of bile, the digestive fluid your liver makes. Bile contains bilirubin, which gives stool its brown color [3], and gut bacteria help process these pigments along the way [4].

A green bowel movement happens for one of two broad reasons:

1. **Something green (or blue) is in the stool.** Pigments from food or dyes can tint it [1].
2. **Bile didn't have time to turn brown.** When stool passes through quickly, it can stay greenish. Cleveland Clinic lists "food passing through your intestines too fast" as a health cause of green stool [2].

## What causes a green bowel movement?

Here are the usual causes, from most to least likely [1][2]:

| Cause | Examples | How long it lasts | Concerning? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Green foods** | Spinach, kale, broccoli; avocado, green apples, honeydew; basil, cilantro, parsley; matcha; pistachios; hemp seeds | A day or two | No |
| **Blue or purple foods** | Blueberries and other blue/purple produce | A day or two | No |
| **Food dye** | Green frosting, colored drinks; blue or purple dyes can also turn stool greenish | Until the dye has passed | No |
| **Fast transit** | Diarrhea from any cause | As long as the diarrhea | Depends on the cause |
| **Medications** | Antibiotics can change gut bacteria; some medicines cause bile-tinged diarrhea | During/after the course | Usually not; mention it if diarrhea is severe |
| **Infections** | *Salmonella*, *E. coli*, norovirus, *Giardia* | Varies | Yes, if with fever, pain or ongoing diarrhea |
| **GI conditions** | IBS, ulcerative colitis, Crohn's disease; after gallbladder removal | Recurs | Worth discussing with a doctor |

A Cleveland Clinic gastroenterologist sums it up: these medical causes are possible, "but they're not the norm" [1].

## How can you figure out your cause in three questions?

Work through these in order. Most people have their answer by question 1.

**1. Did you eat or drink anything green, blue, purple or brightly dyed in the last 1–2 days?**
Food takes about a day or more to pass through most adults [6], so think back to yesterday, not just today. If yes, food is the likely cause. Expect brown again within a day or two [1].

**2. Is the stool loose (Bristol type 6 or 7)?**
If yes, fast transit is likely part of the story, and the real question is what's causing the loose stool. See [Bristol types 5, 6 and 7](/blog/bristol-stool-type-6). If it's formed (type 3–4) and you feel fine, it's very likely dietary [1].

**3. Have you started a new medicine, especially an antibiotic, or do you have fever, cramps or vomiting?**
Medicines can explain it. Fever, significant pain or vomiting with green diarrhea raise the chance of an infection, so call a doctor if those symptoms persist [1][7].

**Quick decision table**

| Ate green/dyed food? | Loose stool? | Other symptoms? | Likely explanation | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Yes | No | No | Food or dye | Nothing; wait 1–2 days |
| No | No | No | Food you've forgotten, or normal variation | Log it; see if it repeats |
| Either | Yes | No | Fast transit | Hydrate; log loose stools; see a doctor if it lasts more than 2 days [7] |
| Either | Yes | Fever, pain, vomiting, blood | Possible infection or other cause | Contact a doctor |
| No | Either | Keeps happening for weeks | Possibly a GI condition | Book an appointment; bring a log |

## Does dark green or light green mean something different?

Not reliably. The shade depends on what you ate and how fast things moved. Bright or light green often follows dyes or fast, loose stools. Dark green often follows lots of leafy greens or blue/purple foods.

One important exception: if stool looks so dark green it's almost **black**, or it's black and tarry with a strong smell, treat it as black stool, not green. Black stool can come from iron supplements or bismuth (Pepto-Bismol), but tarry black stool can also be a sign of bleeding in the upper digestive tract [5]. ## When should you worry about green poop?

Green poop on its own, when you feel well, is rarely a concern. Contact a doctor if green stool [1]:

- Lasts or keeps coming back without a food explanation
- Comes with **abdominal pain, weight loss, fever, vomiting or bleeding**
- Comes with diarrhea lasting more than 2 days, six or more loose stools a day, or signs of dehydration [7]

Also see a doctor promptly for:
- **Blood** in your stool, or stool that is black and tarry or dark red [8]
- **Pale** or clay-colored stool, which Cleveland Clinic flags as a particular concern [1]
- A change in bowel habit lasting more than a couple of weeks, or unintended weight loss [8]
- New symptoms after age 50, or a family history of colorectal cancer, IBD or celiac disease

## FAQ

**Is it normal to have green poop?**
Yes, occasionally. "The color and shade of a bowel movement can vary day by day," notes a Cleveland Clinic gastroenterologist [1]. Green that follows green or dyed food and fades within a day or two is normal.

**Why is my poop green when I haven't eaten anything green?**
Check for blue or purple foods and dyed drinks or candy, which can also turn stool green [1]. If there's nothing, faster transit or a recent antibiotic are common explanations. Persistent green with other symptoms deserves a doctor's visit.

**Can stress make my poop green?**
Stress can speed up the gut for some people, and faster transit can leave stool greenish [2]. If you notice green, loose stools on stressful days, log both and look for the pattern.

**Can iron supplements make poop green?**
Iron supplements more typically make stool black [2][5]. If your stool is very dark green to black and you take iron, mention it to your doctor or pharmacist. If it's tarry or you feel dizzy, seek care promptly.

**How long does green poop last?**
Food-related green usually fades within a day or two [1]. Green from diarrhea lasts as long as the diarrhea does.
