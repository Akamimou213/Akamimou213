> **Quick answer:** Tell your doctor six things about your bowel movements: how often you go (now vs before), what they look like (the Bristol Stool Scale type, 1–7), their color, how it feels to go (urgency, straining, incomplete emptying, accidents), anything unusual (blood, mucus, black or pale stool), and when the change started. Lead with any blood or black stool. Numbers from a 1–2 week log beat "it's been weird lately".

Most people would rather talk about anything else. But "my bowels have been off" is the description doctors hear most, and it's the hardest to act on. The good news: doctors talk about poop all day, there's a standard vocabulary, and once you use it the conversation gets short and specific.

Here's that vocabulary, a script you can read from your phone, and an example log that turns a week of bathroom trips into a two-line answer.

## Key takeaways

- Use the Bristol Stool Scale (types 1–7) instead of "loose" or "hard". It's standard in GI care, and stool form tracks with how fast stool moves through your gut [1].
- Describe the *change*: normal ranges from about three bowel movements a week to three a day [2], so your baseline matters.
- Blood comes first. A Cedars-Sinai gastroenterologist calls it "the first thing you look for in your stool" [3].
- Black, tarry stool can mean bleeding higher up in the gut [4]. Pale or clay-colored stool can point to a bile problem [5]. Mention either one promptly.
- Bring numbers from a log: days, counts and types.

## What does a doctor want to know about your bowel movements?

A doctor wants to know what's changed from your normal, how much, for how long, and whether there are warning signs. In clinical terms that's your "bowel habit history". It usually covers:

| Topic | What to say |
|---|---|
| Frequency | Times per day or week, now vs before |
| Form | Bristol type(s), and the mix |
| Color | Any color that isn't some shade of brown |
| Passage | Urgency, straining, incomplete emptying, accidents, pain when you go |
| Additions | Blood (bright red, dark, on paper or mixed in), mucus, undigested food, oily or floating stool |
| Shape/size | Narrower than usual? [6] |
| Timeline | When it started, sudden or gradual, constant or on and off |
| Context | New medicines, diet changes, travel, illness, your menstrual cycle |

## How do you describe stool shape and consistency?

Use the Bristol Stool Scale. It's a seven-point chart of stool form that clinicians use as shorthand, from type 1 (separate hard lumps) to type 7 (entirely liquid). Lower numbers generally mean slower transit through the gut, higher numbers faster [1].

| Bristol type | Looks like | Plain-language version |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Separate hard lumps, like nuts | "Hard pellets" |
| 2 | Sausage-shaped but lumpy | "Lumpy and hard" |
| 3 | Sausage with cracks on the surface | "Formed, a bit dry" |
| 4 | Smooth, soft sausage or snake | "Normal, easy to pass" |
| 5 | Soft blobs with clear-cut edges | "Soft pieces" |
| 6 | Fluffy, mushy pieces with ragged edges | "Mushy" |
| 7 | Watery, no solid pieces | "Liquid" |

Types 3–4 are generally considered the ideal range, types 1–2 suggest constipation and types 6–7 loose stools. Give the mix, not one number: "Mostly 6, sometimes 7, the odd 4." See the full [Bristol stool chart](/resources/bristol-stool-chart) for pictures.

## How do you describe frequency, color and how it feels to go?

### Frequency

Give real numbers and a comparison: "Usually once a day; for six weeks it's been three or four times a day." In a study that screened out gut disease, healthy adults ranged from three bowel movements a week to three a day [2]. So whether you're "normal" depends on your own baseline. NIDDK lists fewer than three bowel movements a week among constipation symptoms [7], and describes diarrhea as loose, watery stools three or more times a day, or more often than is normal for you [8].

### Color

Most shades of brown are normal, and food can shift color. The colors to mention specifically:

| Color | Possible meaning | What to tell your doctor |
|---|---|---|
| **Black, tarry** | Often bleeding in the upper gut (melena). Iron tablets, bismuth (Pepto-Bismol), black licorice or blueberries can also cause it [4] | "Black and sticky, and I'm not taking iron or Pepto." Mention promptly. |
| **Bright red streaks or blood** | More often bleeding lower down: colon, rectum or anus [4]. Beets and red foods can mimic it [4] | Say whether it's on the paper, in the bowl or mixed in. |
| **Pale, clay or putty-colored** | Possible bile-flow problem involving the liver, gallbladder or pancreas [5] | "Pale for several days", and mention dark urine or yellow skin. |
| Green or yellow | Often diet or fast transit | Mention if it's persistent, or comes with diarrhea or greasy stool |

Black stool is worth taking seriously. A Cedars-Sinai gastroenterologist says "black poop is never normal", while noting that iron pills and some over-the-counter stomach medicines can cause it [3].

### How it feels to go

These words help your doctor tell different problems apart:

- **Urgency:** "I have to run when I need to go."
- **Straining:** "I push hard even when it's soft."
- **Incomplete emptying:** "I feel like I haven't finished." A persistent urge that isn't relieved by going is listed among colorectal cancer symptoms [6].
- **Accidents/leakage:** say so plainly. Doctors hear it often, and it changes the workup.
- **Pain:** before, during or after? Relieved by going? Our guide on [how to describe abdominal pain](/blog/how-to-describe-abdominal-pain) helps here.
- **Night-time:** "I wake up needing to go." Always mention this one.

## What should you say word for word?

Use this script, filled in with your own numbers. Put red flags first.

> "I'm here because my bowel habits have changed. **[Red flags first: 'I've seen blood twice' / 'No blood, no black stool, no weight loss.']**
> It started around **[month]**, **[suddenly / gradually]**, **[after X, if anything]**.
> Before, I went **[frequency]**, usually Bristol type **[number]**. Now I go **[frequency]**, mostly type **[number]**, sometimes **[number]**.
> I **[do / don't]** have urgency, straining, a feeling of not finishing, or accidents.
> The color is **[usual brown / describe]**. I **[have / haven't]** seen mucus.
> It **[does / doesn't]** wake me at night.
> I've tried **[X]**, and **[result]**."

If saying it out loud feels awkward, hand over a printed version. Our [GI symptom checklist](/blog/gi-symptom-checklist) has these fields ready to fill in.

## How do you build your bowel habit history?

Keep a log for 1–2 weeks (longer if your symptoms come and go), then summarize it. Recording each bowel movement as it happens is far more accurate than trying to remember a month of bathroom trips.

**Log each time:** time, Bristol type, color if unusual, urgency/straining/incomplete emptying, blood or mucus, pain. **Log each day:** no-bowel-movement days, meals, medicines and supplements, and notes such as sleep, stress, period or travel.

**Illustrative 7-day log** (example data, not a real patient):

| Day | BMs | Bristol types | Urgency | Blood/mucus | Night | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | 3 | 6, 6, 5 | 2 of 3 | –/– | No | — |
| Tue | 4 | 7, 6, 6, 6 | 3 of 4 | –/mucus | No | Coffee ×3 |
| Wed | 2 | 5, 6 | 1 of 2 | –/– | No | — |
| Thu | 3 | 6, 6, 6 | 2 of 3 | –/– | No | Takeout dinner |
| Fri | 3 | 6, 5, 6 | 1 of 3 | –/– | No | — |
| Sat | 2 | 5, 4 | 0 | –/– | No | Day off |
| Sun | 2 | 5, 5 | 0 | –/– | No | — |

**Turned into a two-line answer:**
> "Over 7 days: 19 bowel movements, about 2–4 a day. Mostly Bristol 6 (11 of 19), urgency in 9, mucus once, no blood, no night symptoms. Better at weekends."

## When to see a doctor

Don't save these for a routine visit. Contact a doctor promptly if you notice:

- Blood in or on your stool, or bleeding from your bottom [6]
- Black, tarry stool not explained by iron, bismuth or food [4]
- Pale or clay-colored stools for several days, especially with dark urine or yellowing skin [5]
- A change in bowel habits, such as diarrhea, constipation or narrower stools, that doesn't settle [6]
- A persistent urge to go that isn't relieved by going [6]
- Constipation with constant belly pain, vomiting, fever, being unable to pass gas, or weight loss [7]
- Diarrhea lasting more than 2 days, six or more loose stools a day, high fever, or signs of dehydration [8]
- Symptoms that wake you at night, unexplained weight loss, or a family history of colorectal cancer, IBD or celiac disease

## FAQ

**Is it embarrassing to talk to a doctor about poop?**
It may feel that way, but bowel habits are routine for doctors, especially gastroenterologists. Plain words are fine. "Mushy", "pellets" and "I had an accident" are all useful descriptions.

**Should I bring a photo of my stool to the doctor?**
Ask first. Some clinicians find a photo useful for something unusual, such as color or blood, but a Bristol type and a clear description usually do the job. If you take photos, store them somewhere private.

**What's a "bowel habit history"?**
It's the part of a medical history about your bowel movements: frequency, form, color, how it feels to go, any blood or mucus, when things changed, and what might be related. The script above covers it.

**How do I describe diarrhea vs loose stool to my doctor?**
Use Bristol types and counts. "Type 6, three times a day" and "type 7, six times a day" mean very different things. NIDDK describes diarrhea as loose, watery stools three or more times a day, or more often than is normal for you [8].
