# How Get Regular sounds

Read this before writing anything for Get Regular: Learn articles, tools, CTAs, App Store copy, outreach or social. The facts live in `research/product-walkthrough.md` and `research/product-fact-sheet.md`. This file is about **how we say them**.

The voice comes from the product itself. These are real lines from the app, site and creatives:

> "Your gut talks. We help you understand it so that you can finally trust it."
> "You've been flushing your most powerful biomarker."
> "Smooth move." · "Don't flush your progress." · "Send me gut tips worth reading on the toilet."
> "Text your gut. It reads every log."
> "The report your doctor wishes everyone brought."
> "Meals your gut agreed to in advance."
> "Invite your TMI friends." · "Smelly Farts" (a real symptom tag)
> "Dairy Elimination · No change · *Nothing happened, I think I can have cheese.*"
> "Memory fades. *Keep the details.*" · "Healthy food. *Different reactions.*" · "Let's make *poop* less awkward."

## 1. Who we're talking to

Someone who has felt "off" for a while (bloated, irregular, tired, breaking out) and has been told it's stress, or "just IBS", or nothing at all. They've Googled at 1 a.m., tried cutting things, and still don't know what's going on. Many are women; many care about skin, hair, energy and mood as much as digestion. They want to understand their own body, not be lectured, sold supplements or scared.

From the reviews, they hire us to:
- **make tracking feel easy** ("usually gets overwhelmed by tracking");
- **get permission to take it seriously** ("my symptoms aren't normal and I can see a doctor without thinking I'm crazy");
- **see the connections** between food, sleep, mood, cycle and gut;
- **not be reduced to one diagnosis** ("doesn't reduce every bit of discomfort to your autoimmune condition").

## 2. Four traits

| We are | Which means | Not |
|---|---|---|
| **Candid about poop** | We name it: poop, farts, "did you go?". A little wit takes the awkwardness out | Crude, or so clinical it's cold ("bowel elimination events") |
| **On your side** | "You've been guessing for years. This is where it stops." Validate first, then help | Preachy, blaming ("you're doing it wrong") or fear-based ("your gut is toxic") |
| **Specific** | "Your fiber averaged 5 g a day against 25–30 g." Numbers, foods, days | Vague wellness ("optimize your gut journey", "unlock your potential") |
| **Honest about limits** | Patterns, not diagnoses. "Talk to a doctor" when it matters. iOS only, said plainly | Overclaiming ("clinically validated", "find the root cause", "cure") |

## 3. Rhythm and craft

- **Short lines, then one that lands.** "Memory fades. Keep the details." Two beats: the problem, then the turn.
- **One emphasized word**, set in italic in design: "less *awkward*", "what *works for you*", "how *you're feeling*". Never more than one per line.
- **Concrete over abstract:** "a week of breakfasts and how you felt after" beats "your dietary inputs".
- **Lead with the person, then the feature:** "See what changed the days you felt off" (benefit), then "symptom tags" (feature).
- **Puns sparingly**, about one per screen or email, and only where they're natural ("Smooth move", "Don't flush your progress"). Never in safety or medical content.
- **"You" and "your gut".** "We" is the team. Never "users" or "patients" in consumer copy.

## 4. Our words

| Use | Instead of |
|---|---|
| poop, stool (in medical context), bowel movement, "did you go?" | feces, elimination, BM (in headings) |
| patterns, connections, what lines up | root cause, diagnosis, detect |
| try one change, Try This, experiment | protocol, treatment, cure |
| your gut profile, your logs, your data | your health record, inputs |
| share with your doctor, Doctor Report | medical-grade, clinical report |
| feel off, bloated, irregular | dysbiosis, toxins, "leaky gut" (unless we're debunking it) |

**Product names (always capitalised):** Get Regular · Gut Check · Try This (feature) / experiments (generic) · the Digest, Weekly Digest, Daily Digest · Doctor Report · Gut Health Score, Nutrition Score, Regular Score · Gut Streak · TMI Friends · Backlog.

## 5. By surface

**Learn articles and Science pages (SEO, AEO, GEO).** Lead with the answer, precise and sourced (`blogs/_EDITORIAL_STANDARD.md`). The voice shows in the intro, the subheads and the closing tip, not in the medical facts. Example: an H2 "When to see a doctor" stays plain; the intro can open "If you've been staring into the bowl wondering whether that's normal, here's how to tell."

**Free tools.** Warm and quick: "Tap the type that looks most like yours." The result is plain and safe. The CTA carries the personality: "One poop is a clue. A week is the story."

**CTAs.** Name the next small action and why it matters to *them*:
- "Start your 7-day plan in Get Regular" (not "Download now")
- "Log a week and see your pattern"
- "Bring the report your doctor wishes everyone brought"

**Outreach to editors.** Same honesty, less wit. Lead with what's useful for *their* readers, give specifics they can verify, state limits up front, one light line at most. No hype words.

**App Store and social.** The fullest version of the voice: the creatives' two-beat headlines and one underlined CTA.

## 6. Before and after

| Generic (don't) | Get Regular (do) |
|---|---|
| "Get Regular is an AI-powered gut-health tracking app that provides personalized insights." | "Get Regular keeps the details you'd otherwise forget (what you ate, how you went, how you felt) and shows you what lines up." |
| "Log meals, bowel movements and symptoms to see daily scores." | "Snap a meal, tap your poop type, add how you feel. Ten seconds, and your scores update." |
| "Guided experiments to identify triggers." | "Try one change for a week, like dairy-free mornings, and see what actually happens. Sometimes the answer is: you can have the cheese." |

| "Weekly AI insights based on your data." | "Every week: what's trending, what's shifting, and the one nutrient you keep missing." |
| "Download the app to start tracking." | "Log a week. See your pattern." |

## 7. Never

- Health claims we can't back: "clinically validated", "diagnose", "detect disease", "cure", "FDA", "root cause".
- Fear or shame: "toxic", "your gut is broken", "you're doing it wrong".
- Diet-culture framing: calories are optional in the app ("helps some people and stresses others out"), and our copy treats them that way.
- Fake urgency or social proof: no "thousands of users" without a dated number, no invented reviews.
- Jokes about red flags: blood, black stool, weight loss and night symptoms are always plain and serious.
