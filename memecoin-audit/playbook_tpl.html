Memecoin trading system · v1 · 25 Oct 2026

Trench Playbook

A complete operating system for trading Solana and EVM memecoins: how the market works, which tools to use in which role, how to research and chart a coin, how to set up and manage the trade, and how to stay solvent while you find out whether you have an edge.

Every rule is tagged by how much evidence stands behind it: data tested on the 148-trade on-chain backtest from the Gambles audit, source taken from protocol docs or published research, rule of thumb common practice that hasn't been tested here. Results are from one channel's calls, May–Oct 2026, and are in-sample. They are hypotheses to confirm with your own trades, not guarantees.

00 · The system on one card

Six rules that decide every trade

Gate · data

Only enter if the coin traded ≥ $50k (ideally ≥ $117k) in the last 30 minutes and price sits ≥ 7% below its 60-minute high. Never buy within 7% of the recent high.

Window · data

Trade hardest 00:00–08:00 UTC (convert to your local time). Outside it, take only coins that pass the gate cleanly, at half size.

Safety · source

Every coin passes the 90-second dossier: contract, liquidity, holders, wallets. One failed check means skip.

Exit · data

Sell 100% at 2x from your fill. Stop at −50%. If neither has hit after 60 minutes, sell. Never hold overnight.

Size · math

Risk 0.5–1% of bankroll per trade. Position = bankroll × risk% ÷ stop%. No more than 5 positions open.

Review · math

Log every trade. After 50 trades, if average return after fees is ≤ 0, stop and diagnose. A 15% monthly drawdown ends the month.

Backtest at a 4.5% round-trip cost. Coins passing the volume + pullback gate: +24.6% per trade, 54% wins (n=41, 90% range +7% to +42%). Calls posted 00–08 UTC: +35% (n=28). Buying within 7% of the 60-minute high: −16.4% (n=50). Every call, no filter: +4.3%, which is within noise of break-even.

01 · Mindset

You are the liquidity until you prove otherwise

The memecoin market moves money from people who don't have a process to the people who do. Before trading, accept these numbers: they describe the default outcome you're trying to escape.

90%of 829,539 Fomo-app wallets were behind after fees (Jan–Oct 2026). The top 1% took 94% of all winnings.
$579Mmade by pump.fun devs and their bundles, vs $31.5M by sniper bots (Oct 2025–Jun 2026)
80%of pump.fun tokens stop trading by day one. 4.55% still trade after 90 days.
48%chance of a run of 8+ straight losses in 100 trades at a 40% win rate
Think in expectancy

Win rate only matters against the payoff

With a 2x target and a −50% stop, a win nets +91% and a loss −52% after a 4.5% round-trip cost. You break even at about 36% wins. Each extra point of win rate above that is your edge. A 3x target needs 22% wins, and the calls managed 19%. That's why "hold for the moon" loses.

Expect streaks

Losing streaks are normal, not a signal

At a 40% win rate, the median worst streak in 100 trades is 7 losses in a row, and there's a 20% chance of 10. With 1% risk per trade, ten losses cost about 10% of the bankroll. That's survivable. At 10% risk per trade it would wipe out about two-thirds of it. Position size is what keeps you in the game.

Banned behaviours

The habits that turn a small edge into a loss

  • Chasing a coin that already pumped ("I'll just catch the next leg").
  • Moving a stop down, or removing it, once in a trade.
  • Averaging down on a coin that hit your stop.
  • Revenge trading after a loss, or sizing up after a win streak.
  • Holding past the time stop "because the community is strong".
  • Trading outside your plan because a KOL tweeted.
Operating habits

What disciplined operators do

  • Decide the exit before the entry, and let the platform execute it.
  • Treat every skip as a win: there will be another coin in five minutes.
  • Keep the trading bankroll in a separate hot wallet and withdraw profits weekly.
  • Judge yourself on process, meaning whether you followed the rules, not on single outcomes.
  • Stop for the day after 3 consecutive rule breaks or a 5% daily drawdown.
02 · Market mechanics

How a memecoin is born, priced and killed

Lifecycle on pump.fun source. A creator mints 1B tokens. 793.1M of them sit on a bonding curve, a price formula with virtual reserves of 30 SOL and 1.073B tokens. Every buy raises the price along the curve. When all 793.1M curve tokens are sold (about 85 SOL of real buying), the coin graduates. The SOL raised plus the remaining ~206.9M tokens seed a PumpSwap pool, and the pool's LP tokens are burned. From then on the price is set by the pool.

Stage 1LaunchDev creates the coin; snipers and dev bundles buy in block 0 on 28–38% of coins.
Stage 2Bonding curve1.25% fee per trade. "Progress %" = share of 793.1M tokens sold. Most coins die here: about 1% graduate.
Stage 3Graduation~85 SOL + ~206.9M tokens move to PumpSwap, LP burned. Often a volatile moment: early holders sell into new buyers.
Stage 4AMM tradingPrice = pool ratio. Fees tier down from 1.25% as mcap grows. Calls and boosts drive flow.
Stage 5Decay98% of called coins end below the channel's entry price. 79% are delisted from DexScreener within months.

How a pool prices your trade source

A pool holds two reserves, SOL (x) and token (y), and keeps x × y constant. Buying adds SOL and removes tokens, so each token you take costs more than the last. That cost is price impact. It depends only on your size relative to the pool. Slippage tolerance is different: it's the worst fill you'll accept. Any gap between the two is room for a sandwich bot.

Fees you actually pay · source
  • pump.fun curve: 1.25% per trade (0.95% protocol, 0.30% creator).
  • PumpSwap: 1.25% under 420 SOL mcap, falling by tier to 0.30% at 98,240+ SOL.
  • Terminal: Axiom 1% (0.75–0.95% net with cashback); GMGN, Trojan, Maestro, Photon about 1%.
  • Jupiter Swap V2: 50 bps on tokens under 24 hours old.
  • Network: priority fee plus Jito tip, about 0.001–0.005 SOL per transaction.
  • Total round trip: about 4–5% on a young coin. Budget 4.5%.
Sandwich attacks · source

A bot buys just before you, lets your order fill at your worst accepted price, then sells into you. Between Oct 2025 and Oct 2026, 6.85M sandwiches took $29.7M, and 96% of them hit pump.fun coins. Swaps allowing 20–50% slippage were sandwiched 14 times as often as swaps under 3%. A terminal's "MEV protect" switch helps but only blocks part of the attack paths.

Defence: buy slippage of 10% or less (3–5% on bonded coins with deep pools), Secure MEV mode, and split buys over 2% of pool liquidity.

"LP burned" is not a safety rating. The most common Solana rug in 2026 is a dump into burned liquidity: the dev sells supply they bought cheaply on the curve. On pump.fun, the LP burn is automatic for every coin, so it tells you nothing about the dev. Holders and wallets are where the risk is.

Contract authorities source

Solana
  • Mint authority live means unlimited new supply can be printed. Must be revoked.
  • Freeze authority live means your tokens can be frozen so you can't sell. Must be revoked.
  • Mutable metadata lets the name, ticker or image change. Low risk alone, but a yellow flag.
  • Token-2022 extensions to reject: PermanentDelegate (can move or burn your tokens), TransferHook (can block sells), TransferFee (adjustable tax), DefaultAccountState=Frozen, NonTransferable, Pausable.
  • pump.fun coins revoke mint and freeze by design. Other launchpads and custom tokens need checking.
EVM (RBH, BNB, Base, ETH)
  • Honeypot: run a sell simulation (honeypot.is). Reject if the sell fails or cannot_sell_all is set.
  • Taxes: reject a buy or sell tax over 5%, and any owner-modifiable tax (slippage_modifiable).
  • Owner powers: reject hidden_owner, owner_change_balance, blacklist, trading pause or cooldown, and mintable.
  • Proxy (upgradeable) contracts can change their logic later. Reject for memecoins.
  • A simulation shows the state now. Owner-toggled traps can pass it. Renounced ownership plus verified source is the stronger check.

Security rules · source
  • Past incidents: Banana Gun lost $3M from user wallets (Sep 2024). Solareum lost $523k and shut down (Mar 2024). A GMGN phishing attack hit 107 accounts (Oct 2025). Malicious browser extensions stole Axiom and Padre session tokens (Sep 2026).
  • Use a dedicated browser profile with no extensions for trading. Bookmark the official domains and never use search ads.
  • Fund terminal and bot wallets with the day's trading float only. Sweep profits to a cold wallet weekly.
  • Enable 2FA and withdrawal whitelists. Never paste a seed or private key into a "verification" bot. Fake "Safeguard" bots plant malware.
  • Revoke all sessions after installing anything suspicious.
05 · Bots & snipers

Use automation for exits, not for racing the block

"Bot" covers four different things. Only two of them help a retail trader.

Bot typeWhat it doesVerdict
Order automation (TP, SL, trailing, time exits, limit, DCA)Executes your pre-set plan the instant price hits a levelUse always. It removes hesitation, and the backtest assumes exits fire on the level.
Alerts & filters (Pulse filters, wallet trackers, tweet monitors)Narrows thousands of launches to the few that meet your gateUse. This is where your speed comes from.
Launch / migration sniper (retail)Fires a buy when a token launches, when a ticker or deployer appears, or at graduationAvoid as an entry strategy. Retail snipers react after the trigger. They don't compete for block 0 with dev bundles.
Copy tradingMirrors a tracked wallet's buys and sellsOnly as alerts, never auto-buy. You fill after them, they can bait you, and Maestro even sells "front-run the copied wallet" as a feature.
How professional sniping works · source

Pros pay to get their transactions into the leader's block first:

  • Jito bundles: up to 5 transactions that execute atomically.
  • Stake-weighted QoS: leaders prioritise traffic from staked validators.
  • Paid senders: Nozomi, Astralane, 0slot, bloXroute and Helius Sender.

App users paid $38.1M to landing services, against $6.0M to Jito. Alpenglow, live on 21 Oct 2026 with 150 ms finality targets, is changing these timing games again.

Why retail sniping loses · source
  • Dev wallets bought in block 0 on 21% of coins, taking a median 10% of supply.
  • Devs and bundles made at least $579M; all sniper bots together made $31.5M, or $15.2M if unsold bags count as zero.
  • Only 54% of 25,882 sniper wallets came out ahead, and the top 10 took $6.7M.
  • On 65% of coins the dev sold within one minute. Block 0 is the dev's exit, not your entry.

Automations to set up on day one rule of thumb

  • Buy preset "Gate": fixed size, buy slippage 8–10%, priority fee 0.002–0.005 SOL, MEV Secure.
  • Sell preset "Stop": sell slippage 25–30% so a stop actually fills in a crash.
  • Exit bundle: attached to every buy. TP sells 100% at +100%. SL sells 100% at −50%. Time exit after 60 minutes (on platforms without a time trigger, set a phone timer).
  • Dev-sell alert: GMGN or Terminal "DEV_SELL" trigger to get out when the creator sells.
  • Wallet alerts: 10–20 vetted wallets (see the vetting rules in section 06), as notifications only.
06 · Research a coin

The 90-second dossier

Run these in order. The first failure ends the dossier; you don't need to finish it. The time budget is what lets you still enter inside two minutes of spotting the coin.

  1. Gate (10 s) data

    • 30-minute volume ≥ $50k (≥ $117k preferred).
    • Price ≥ 7% below its 60-minute high. If it's at the high, skip or wait.
    • Inside your trading window, or half size.

  2. Contract (15 s) source

    • Know the launchpad from the CA suffix and the creating program: …pump pump.fun, …bonk Bonk.fun, …4444 four.meme, …7777 / …8888 Flap. Suffixes can be faked by grinding, so check the program.
    • Solana: mint and freeze revoked, no dangerous Token-2022 extensions (RugCheck).
    • EVM: sell simulation passes, tax ≤ 5%, no owner powers, not a proxy.

  3. Liquidity (10 s) source rule of thumb

    • LP burned or permanently locked for the venue (section 03 table).
    • Liquidity at least ~10% of market cap. Below that, your exit will cost more than your entry.
    • Your size ≤ 1–2% of pool liquidity, so impact stays around 2–4% each way.

  4. Holders (25 s) rule of thumb

    • Top 10 holders (excluding pool and curve) ≤ 30%.
    • Dev holds ≤ 5%. Dev's past coins aren't rugs (GMGN dev history).
    • Bundle % ≤ 10–15%. Snipers % low or falling. Insiders % low.
    • Fresh-wallet share is not dominating the top holders.
    • Bubblemaps: no large linked cluster outside known pools and exchanges.

  5. Wallets & narrative (20 s) rule of thumb

    • Smart-degen or vetted wallets are holding or adding, not selling into the pump.
    • The meme is the original, not a copycat ticker of something already pumped.
    • X account and community are real: account age, replies from real accounts, a clear story.
    • Call or KOL attention just started. Treat CTOs, presales and re-posted calls as lower quality, per the channel data.
Vetting a "smart wallet" before you track it
  • At least 50 closed positions over 30 days. Look at the median trade, not just total PnL.
  • No more than half of its PnL from one or two trades. That's luck, not edge.
  • Average win ≥ 1.5× average loss. A high win rate with small wins and large holds loses.
  • Check who funded it (Birdeye funded-by, GMGN fund_from). Deployer-funded wallets are bait.
  • Re-score monthly; edge decays.
07 · Chart reading

What the chart actually tells you on a memecoin

Classic indicators were built for markets with thousands of participants and no single seller holding 10% of supply. On a memecoin chart, the useful information is who is trading, how much, and where price sits against its recent range. I tested six pre-entry chart features on the 148-trade on-chain backtest.

Feature at entryResultAvg per trade (after 3% cost)Use
30-min volume > $117k vs < $19kStrong+19.8% vs −6.5%Gate: participation is everything
Price vs 60-min high: within 7% vs 7–20% belowStrong−15.0% vs +17.6%Gate: never buy the top of the spike

Deep dump (limit at 0.7× / 0.5× of the signal price)Fails≈0% / −19%Coins that dump 30%+ usually keep going

Read in this order
  1. Timeframe: 1-minute for entries, 5-minute for context. Use 1-second only to watch fills.
  2. Volume bars: is participation rising into the pullback, or dying? A falling price on falling volume is a pause. On rising volume it's distribution.
  3. Range position: mark the 60-minute high and the pre-pump base. Buy the 7–20% pullback from the high, not the breakout candle.
  4. Structure: higher lows on 1-minute after the first spike mean buyers are absorbing. A clean break of the last higher low means the setup is invalid.
  5. Round-number mcap levels ($100k, $250k, $500k, $1M) act as magnets and sell zones rule of thumb. Place your 2x target just below one, not above it.
Read the transaction feed, not just candles
  • Who's selling: sells from the dev, bundle wallets or a top-5 holder override any chart pattern. Exit or skip.
  • Identical-size buys repeating every few seconds are a volume bot. Discount that volume.
  • Unique makers vs volume: $200k volume from 40 wallets is wash trading. 82.8% of tokens up more than 100% showed artificial growth (2026 study).
  • Graduation: expect a volatile minute at migration as curve buyers take profit. Don't market-buy into it.
  • Buy/sell count should hold up through the pullback (many small buyers), not collapse.
08 · Trade setup

From signal to filled order, step by step

  1. Size the position

    Position = bankroll × risk% ÷ stop distance. Example: $2,000 bankroll, 1% risk, 50% stop → $40 position, risking $20. Check that $40 is under 2% of pool liquidity. If it isn't, shrink it.

  2. Choose the entry type

    • Gate passes now (high volume, already 7–20% off the high): market buy with the "Gate" preset.
    • At the high: skip. Optionally place a limit 10–15% below the 60-minute high, expiring in 60 minutes untested, rule of thumb. Cancel it if volume dries up.
    • Already down 30%+ from the high: skip. Deep dumps kept falling in the backtest.

  3. Attach exits on fill data

    Exit planAvg per trade, all 148 calls (4.5% cost)Use when
    TP 2x · SL −50%+4.3%Default for A-setups
    TP 2x · SL −40% · 60-min time stop+4.1%Default when you can't watch the screen
    TP 1.5x · SL −30%−5.7%Don't use. Stops too tight for memecoin noise.
    TP 3x or 5x−8% / −6%Don't use. Too few coins get there before collapsing.
    Hold 7 days−66.8%Never

    These are unfiltered averages over all calls. The gain comes from the entry gate (section 07), not from the exit plan.

  4. Manage, don't meddle

    Once the exits are set, your only allowed actions are an emergency exit (dev or bundle sells, a contract flag appears, liquidity is pulled) and the time stop. Never widen the stop, cancel the TP to "let it run", or add to the position.

  5. Log it

    Coin, CA, time, signal source, the price you first saw, fill price and delay, gate values (volume, % off high), dossier notes, exit reason, result after fees.

09 · Daily workflow

A session, start to finish

Pre-session · 10 minPrepareFund the hot wallet with the session float only. Check SOL and market mood. Review yesterday's journal. Confirm presets and exit bundle.
Loop · continuousScanAxiom Pulse with gate filters (volume, mcap, Top 10 ≤ 30%, Dev ≤ 5%, Bundle ≤ 15%); the channel and wallet alerts as extra signals.
Loop · 90 sDossierGate → contract → liquidity → holders → wallets and narrative. The first fail means skip.
Loop · 10 sExecuteSize, buy with the preset, exits attached at once.
Loop · ≤ 60 minManageWatch for dev or bundle sells only. TP, SL or time stop ends the trade.
Post-session · 10 minReviewComplete the journal. Note rule breaks. Sweep profits above the float to cold storage.
Session limits rule of thumb
  • Primary window: 00:00–08:00 UTC. Plan sleep around it, or trade its overlap with your day.
  • Maximum 10 trades per session and 5 open at once.
  • Stop after −5% of bankroll in a day, or 3 rule breaks.
  • No trading tired, drunk or angry. The rules exist because your judgement in those states is worse.
Weekly review
  • Expectancy (average return per trade after fees) vs the 36% break-even win rate.
  • Average fill delay and fill price vs the price at first sight. That gap is your execution cost.
  • Results by setup: A (both gates) vs B (one gate) vs rule breaks.
  • Drop whatever loses over 30+ trades. Keep whatever wins.
