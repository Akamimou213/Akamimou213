Gambles Channel Audit

I checked {{NFRESH}} fresh calls from the channel against on-chain prices. The channel finds coins that move, but its published multiples are measured from a price you cannot get, and 98% of called coins are worth less today than at the call. Copying its calls only breaks even or better if you enter within about a minute, take a fixed 2x and never hold. Even then the edge is thin, except for calls posted 00–08 UTC.

Bottom line
  • Never hold. Buying 1 minute after the call and holding lost {{H16_EV}} over 16 hours and {{HOLD_EV}} over 7 days.
  • A fast entry with a fixed 2x exit roughly breaks even. Buying 1 minute in with TP 2x / SL −50% averaged {{BC_EV}} per trade after costs (90% interval {{BC_LO}} to {{BC_HI}}, n={{NS}}). The win rate was {{BC_WIN}}, against 35% needed to break even. Entering at +5 minutes instead dropped the average to {{D5_EV}}. Further targets (3x, 5x) and tighter 1.5x exits did worse.
  • Time of day is the strongest filter. Calls posted 00–08 UTC averaged {{U_EV}} with the same rule (interval {{U_LO}} to {{U_HI}}, n={{U_N}}) and were positive in every month sampled. Separately, the channel's own data across 3,777 calls also shows 00–04 UTC calls reaching 5x more often. The sample is small and I tested several slices, so trade it small until your own log confirms it.
  • The channel's scoreboard is inflated. Its "Nx" figures start from a mcap below the real price at the moment of posting, typically {{RQ}}× of it. Measured from the real post-time price, the median sampled coin peaked at {{M15}} within 15 minutes and {{M7D}} within 7 days. The channel reported {{CLM}} for the same coins.
  • Two ideas that looked good on a smaller sample failed on the full one. Buying the dip at 0.5–0.7× the call price and skipping coins under $50k both looked positive at n=65; at n={{NS}} neither held up. Narrative labels (dog, cat, Elon, AI) predict nothing.
01 · The data

What the channel posts

The export holds {{NMSG}} messages. There are three types. A call has the chain, name, a one-line thesis, links and the contract address. A follow-up replies to a call with "7x plus, 500k 🔥 now 270k", meaning the multiple, the peak mcap and the current mcap. A daily digest lists the best performers and says "nothing under 3x counted". I linked every follow-up back to its call through Telegram's reply chain. Only calls with ≥3 days of follow-up time count toward hit rates.

02 · Claimed vs real

The scoreboard starts from a price you can't buy

Taken at face value, the channel's own follow-ups say {{C2}} of fresh calls hit 2x, {{C5}} hit 5x and {{C10}} hit 10x. To test that, I pulled 1-minute candles from GeckoTerminal for a random sample of {{NS}} Solana calls from May to October 2026. The public API serves only the last 180 days. I then measured each coin's peak from the price at the second the call was posted, and again from a realistic follower entry.

The peaks are reported accurately: the channel's peak mcaps line up with the chain. The gap comes from the starting point. The channel's implied entry mcap (peak ÷ multiple) was a median {{RQ}}× the real mcap at posting time (interquartile {{RQ25}}–{{RQ75}}×). Most likely it is the caller's own earlier entry, or the price when the coin was "pinned in chat earlier". Either way it is a price the channel's readers never had.

From the real post-time price, the median coin peaked at {{M15}} within 15 minutes, {{M1H}} within an hour, {{M16}} within 16 hours and {{M7D}} within 7 days.

03 · Timing

The call is the catalyst, and often the top

Half of all peak updates arrive within an hour of the call. The median "2x" is posted {{T2X}} minutes after the call, and a quarter land inside {{T2X25}} minutes. That speed is the channel's own audience buying, alongside snipers who watch the channel and front-run it. After the peak, the lowest "now" figure the channel itself reports is a median {{RETR}} of the peak.

How fast a coin hits 2x does not predict how far it runs: calls that doubled in under 5 minutes and calls that took 4+ hours reached 10x at similar rates (16–21%). Once a call is running, the channel's own data gives these odds of reaching the next rung: 2x→3x {{L23}}, 5x→10x {{L510}}, 10x→20x {{L1020}}. The tail is real, but the channel measures it from its own low reference price.

05 · Patterns

What separates the 5x calls from the rest

I tagged each fresh call by its text, launchpad suffix, links and posting time, then compared the share reaching 5x against all other calls. Dozens of tags were tested, so a few will clear the bar by chance. Treat |z| ≥ 2.6 as the threshold for something real; those are coloured.

Holds up

Established coins beat fresh launches

Calls tagged "just launched" or "low cap" had a median entry of {{JL}}, against {{NJL}} for the rest, and reached 5x much less often (z −8.4). The gap shows up inside every month from January to September, so it is not a seasonal artefact. Calls that include a DexScreener link show the same pattern (z −8.3). Those calls skew young: median entry $30k, and 65% are tagged "just launched". A coin that has already survived to $50–150k has buyers. A coin that launched ten minutes earlier mostly has its dev and the snipers.

Holds up

Late-night UTC and Friday/Saturday

Calls posted 00–04 UTC (z +3.1) and on Fri/Sat (z +4.2) reached 5x more often. A plausible reason is thinner competition from other callers while the US is offline, plus weekend retail flow. This is weaker evidence: it is a timing effect and could fade.

Avoid

CTO, political and re-posted calls

Community-takeover calls (z −2.9 at 5x) and Trump-themed calls did worse. "Update … pinned in chat earlier" re-posts reached 10x at about half the rate of fresh calls (6.5% vs 13.3%). Presale and Pinksale calls were the worst bucket of all: 14% reached 2x.

Noise

Narrative labels

Dog, cat, Elon, AI and "viral/TikTok" sit inside the noise band. The channel's "good branding and ticker" line appears on more than half of all calls and predicts nothing. Narrative decides whether a coin gets called in the first place. It does not help you choose among calls.

06 · Backtest

Trading the calls with real fills

Each rule ran on the {{NS}}-call on-chain sample using 1-minute candles for the first 16 hours and hourly candles out to 7 days. Every trade pays a 3% round-trip haircut for launchpad and AMM fees (about 1% a side), terminal fees and slippage. When a candle hits both stop and target, the stop is assumed to fill first. A stop that gaps through fills at the candle's open. Intervals are 90% bootstrap ranges. With a sample this size, read them as directional.

What the chart says. Holding is a reliable way to lose ({{HOLD_EV}} over 7 days). The fast, fixed-2x rule sits just above zero, and so do the 60-minute time stop ({{T60_EV}}) and the moonbag ({{MOON_EV}}). Tighter 1.5x exits and further 3x and 5x targets do worse. Entering later costs money: the same 2x rule fell from {{BC_EV}} at +1 minute to {{D5_EV}} at +5 minutes.

Dip-buying did not survive the full sample. A limit order at 0.7× the call price filled on {{DIP_FILL}} of calls but averaged {{DIP_EV}}, because coins that dump 30% often keep dumping. At 0.5× the result was clearly negative ({{DIP5_EV}}).

Only one filter changed the outcome: the hour of the call. Calls posted 00–08 UTC averaged {{U_EV}} (n={{U_N}}). Calls at other hours were roughly flat. Market-cap filters gave no consistent pattern on the on-chain sample. The channel-wide finding that micro-caps reach 5x less often stays true, but it doesn't change the result of a 2x trade.

07 · Mechanics

How the memecoin machine works in 2026

Every number in this section comes from the linked source. I checked the CoinGecko, Bitquery and Indiana University figures against the originals myself.

Launchpads and bonding curves

pump.fun: every token has 1B supply, with 793M sold along a bonding curve seeded by virtual reserves of 30 SOL. About 85 SOL of buying completes the curve, and the liquidity then migrates to PumpSwap, pump.fun's own DEX since March 2025. Creator fees are tiered by mcap, from 0.95% under about $300k down to 0.05% above $20M. "Holder Rewards" coins, launched September 2026, pay that fee to holders instead.

Address suffixes tell you the launchpad: …pump is pump.fun, …bonk is Bonk.fun (Raydium LaunchLab), …4444 is four.meme on BNB, and …7777 / 8888 are Flap tax and standard tokens. They are vanity suffixes anyone can grind, so confirm the program that created the token.

Robinhood Chain (RBH): an Arbitrum L2, mainnet since 1 July 2026. Launchpads include Pons, which graduates at 4.2 ETH to a locked Uniswap v4 pool and allows up to a 10% creator tax, and pools.trade. Pons had 1,362 of 124,016 tokens graduate.

pump.fun public docs (GitHub); Blockworks; The Block; Bitquery docs; Robinhood newsroom.

Base rates

Of 18.67M pump.fun tokens, about 1% graduated. 68.7% traded for the last time on launch day, 80.4% were dead by day one, and 4.55% were still trading after 90 days.

On pump.fun, 98.6% of 7M+ tokens fell below $1k liquidity (Solidus Labs, May 2025). Across chains, 3.6% of tokens launched in 2024 showed pump-and-dump patterns (Chainalysis).

As of April 2026, 73% of pump.fun wallets showed some realised profit, but 65% made only $1–500 and 5.4% made more than $1k. Realised PnL leaves out bags still held, so it understates losses.

CoinGecko Research (Jun 2026, May 2026); Solidus Labs; Chainalysis.

Who actually makes the money

Devs and bundles: across 8.2M pump.fun launches (Oct 2025 – Oct 2026), dev wallets bought in the first block on 21% of coins, taking a median 10% of supply. The dev's first sale came within a minute on 65% of coins. Devs and their bundles made at least $579M, against $31.5M for sniper bots.

MEV: 6.85M sandwich attacks took about $29.7M, 96% of it on the pump.fun curve or PumpSwap. Swaps allowing 20–50% slippage were sandwiched about 14 times as often as swaps under 3%.

Wash trading: 82.8% of meme tokens that rose more than 100% showed artificial growth (34,988 tokens across 4 chains).

Bitquery investigations (Oct 2026); arXiv 2507.01963.

How callers get paid

Paid posts: a September 2025 leak showed 160+ KOLs charging $50–60k per post, and fewer than 5 disclosed it. This channel also runs sponsored posts, including recurring MetaWin casino promotions, Pinksale/Cheesepad presales and giveaways.

Front-running: Bubblemaps alleges that wallets linked to one pump.fun caller bought about 20 seconds before 61% of 534 calls and sold about 4 minutes later. That is an allegation, not a ruling.

Platform rewards: pump.fun Callout Rewards (August 2026) pay callers daily in USDC. From October they are scored on whether followers profit.

Track record: no peer-reviewed study finds that followers of call channels profit. Crypto-KOL tweets led to −7.9% over 30 days for small tokens (180 KOLs, 36k tweets), and the effect was worst for self-described experts.

ZachXBT leak via CCN; Bubblemaps via TechFlow; Crypto Briefing; Merkley et al., Review of Accounting Studies 2024.

Legal context: in February 2025 SEC staff said typical meme coins are not securities, which means buyers get no securities-law protection. A RICO class action against pump.fun (Aguilar v. Baton Corp) is moving toward discovery, and the Solana defendants were dismissed on 31 August 2026.

08 · Playbook

How to use this channel without being its exit liquidity

Treat the channel as a deal-flow feed, not a signal. It surfaces about {{FPD}} fresh coins a day that are about to get attention. Any edge has to come from speed, the hour of the call and fixed exits, because the channel's readers are the liquidity that its insiders and the snipers sell into.

  1. Be fast or skip. The rule only breaks even with an entry inside about 1–2 minutes of the post. That means notifications on, the terminal open, and your checks run while the order is being placed. If you see the call after 5 minutes, let it go.
  2. Prioritise calls posted 00–08 UTC, the only slice with a clearly positive result. Outside that window, trade smaller or not at all. Skip presales, prelaunches, CTOs and "pinned in chat earlier" re-posts, which all underperformed in the channel data.
  3. Exit by rule, decided before entry. Sell 100% at 2x from your fill and stop at −50%. If neither has hit after 60 minutes, sell. Never hold overnight: holding 16 hours averaged {{H16_EV}} and 7 days {{HOLD_EV}}.
  4. Run the safety check in parallel (GMGN or Axiom holder tab, RugCheck, Bubblemaps). Skip the coin if any of these hit: dev still holds more than 5% or has a history of rugs; bundled supply above 10–15%; the top 10 wallets excluding the pool hold more than 30%; Bubblemaps shows a cluster of fresh, co-funded wallets; on EVM, mint or freeze authority is live, sell tax is above 5%, or a simulated sell fails. On RBH and Base, check the creator tax.
  5. Keep buy slippage at 10% or less, use MEV protection, and allow wider slippage on stop-loss sells so the stop actually executes in a crash.
  6. Size as if each trade goes to zero. Risk 0.5–1% of your bankroll per trade, with no more than 5 open at once. Set a hard monthly loss limit; 15% is a sensible ceiling. A win rate around 40% means streaks of 6–8 losses will happen.
  7. Keep your own ledger. Log the time, the mcap at the call, your fill, exit and fees for every trade. Review after 50 trades. If your average return after fees is below zero, stop: the edge isn't there at your speed.

09 · Method and limits

What this analysis can and can't tell you

  • Hit rates use the channel's own follow-up posts, which are survivorship-biased. Losers get no follow-up, and deleted messages cannot be inspected.
  • The on-chain sample is {{NS}} randomly drawn Solana fresh calls from May to October 2026. Solana is 79% of calls, and GeckoTerminal's public API stops at 180 days. RBH, ETH and BNB calls were not backtested. Strategy intervals are wide, and a positive result at this sample size is a lead to keep testing, not proof.
  • Fills are modelled from 1-minute candles. Real fills on a coin with a $20k mcap can be worse, through priority-fee wars and failed transactions. The 3% cost haircut is a central estimate, not a worst case.
  • "Dead" means no DexScreener listing or a near-zero mcap on 25 October 2026. A few coins may still trade on venues DexScreener doesn't index.
  • Nothing here is financial advice. Most money in memecoins ends up with devs, bundlers and snipers, and this analysis cannot change that.
